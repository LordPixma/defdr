# DEFDR — Global Defence Sector Landscape: Unmet, Painful, Budgeted Needs

**Date:** September 2026
**Author:** Defence sector research (DEFDR product team)
**Method:** 33 distinct web searches, 17 pages fetched. Primary sources prioritised: UK National Audit Office, US GAO, DoD/DoW Inspector General & Comptroller, Congressional Research Service, NATO, European Defence Agency, European Commission, UK Parliament committees, ANAO, and named regulatory instruments (DFARS, ITAR, Def Stan 05-138).
**Verification standard:** Every figure below is attributed to a URL that was either fetched directly or returned as a live result. Where a page blocked automated fetching and the figure comes only from a search-engine extract, it is marked **[SEARCH-EXTRACT]**. Genuinely unconfirmed items are marked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

---

## 1. Executive summary — ten findings

1. **The money is real, large, and newly arriving.** NATO's Hague summit (24–25 June 2025) committed all allies except Spain to 5% of GDP by 2035, split into "at least 3.5% of GDP to resource core defence requirements" and "up to 1.5% of GDP on broader defence- and security-related investments" ([NATO](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/defence-expenditures-and-natos-5-commitment)). EU-27 defence spending hit **€418bn in 2025, up 20%**, projected **€454bn in 2026** and up to **€547bn by 2029** ([EDA Defence Data via defence-industry.eu](https://defence-industry.eu/eu-defence-spending-jumps-20-to-e418-billion-in-2025-with-budgets-projected-to-approach-e550-billion-by-2029/)).

2. **The single most repeated audit criticism across four countries is not cost — it is *information*.** The NAO's verdict on the MoD's 32 largest programmes (£196.2bn) is blunt: *"The MoD does not have the information it needs to hold teams to account and make strategic decisions."* ([NAO](https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/defence-capabilities-delivering-what-was-promised/)). The UK's own Defence Investment Plan concedes it inherited a portfolio where **47 of 49 major projects were delayed or over budget** [SEARCH-EXTRACT].

3. **Programme timelines are getting worse, not better.** GAO now finds *"the overall average time frame to deliver a capability increased this year to over 12 years"* across 104 programmes worth **over $2.4 trillion**, and that **18 of 40 programmes entered the rapid Middle Tier of Acquisition pathway with immature technologies** ([GAO-26-108457](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108457)).

4. **Nobody can account for the physical stuff.** The MoD manages *"an £11.8 billion portfolio of inventory"* — **640,000 types, 740 million individual items** — on *"two 40-year-old core inventory systems"*, with **105,500m³ of central-warehouse stock "not currently fit for use"** ([NAO](https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/defence-inventory-management/)). The US equivalent: an **eighth consecutive disclaimer of opinion** over **$4.65trn in assets** ([CRS IF12627](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12627.html)).

5. **Munitions demand has gone vertical and the supply chain cannot absorb it.** The FY27 US request is **$70.5bn for missiles — a 188% increase over FY26's $24.4bn**, with PAC-3 MSE going from **357 to 3,203 rounds** and Tomahawk from **55 to 785** ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/04/the-pentagon-wants-a-188-percent-bump-for-missile-procurement-can-industry-deliver/)). Industry's own view, quoted there: *"They can't buy that many in a single year."*

6. **Compliance is now the binding constraint on supplier entry, and regulators have admitted it.** The Department of War suspended CMMC Phase 2 on 13 July 2026, citing *"excessive costs and administrative burdens, especially on small, medium-sized and non-traditional contractors"* that were causing *"innovative companies to leave or avoid the defense industrial base"* ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260720-pentagon-suspends-cmmc-phase-2-requirements-and-launches-review-of-cybersecurity-certification-program)).

7. **A brand-new, unserved compliance regime is landing simultaneously in three places:** the UK's **Defence Cyber Certification (DCC) Level 0 for all suppliers by 31 December 2026**; a proposed **DFARS FOCI rule covering "more than 37,000 entities—including over 21,000 small businesses"** at the $5m threshold ([PilieroMazza](https://www.pilieromazza.com/the-shifting-landscape-for-foreign-ownership-control-or-influence-part-1-understanding-the-proposed-rule/)); and a **new UK "Back British" offsets regime** to be implemented by the National Armaments Director.

8. **Offsets/industrial participation is the largest genuinely under-tooled obligation class in defence.** Over 130 countries operate offset or industrial participation policies, the UK is adding one, and the discipline is still run on spreadsheets and Big-4 consultants (PwC India explicitly markets a bespoke "web-based online application" for it).

9. **Supply-chain risk *illumination* is already a funded, contested market — do not enter it naively.** GSA's **$919m SCRIPTS BPA** (sponsored by OUSD A&S) put Govini, Exiger and Interos in position for a decade ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/govini-selected-for-919-million-supply-chain-risk-illumination-contract-vehicle-from-general-services-administration-302422014.html)). Component-level obsolescence is likewise saturated (SiliconExpert, Z2Data, Accuris).

10. **SMEs are structurally locked out and governments have set hard, measurable targets to fix it.** Only **5% of MoD direct procurement spend went to SMEs in 2024**, with **the top five suppliers taking over 29% of all MOD procurement spending in 2025** and **half of the top-10 frameworks containing fewer than 20 suppliers** ([Tussell](https://www.tussell.com/insights/defence-procurement-tracker)). MoD has committed to **+£2.5bn of SME spend by summer 2028**.

## 2. Pain point catalogue

### PP-01 — Programme portfolio truth: leaders cannot see the real status of their own programmes

- **Pain:** Defence ministries run multi-billion portfolios on reporting they know to be incomplete and lagging. Delivery confidence, schedule and cost data arrive quarterly, manually assembled, and are optimistic by construction.
- **Owner:** Senior Responsible Owners (SROs); Director General Finance / CFO of an MoD; the new UK **National Armaments Director** organisation; OUSD(A&S) portfolio management; Service acquisition executives.
- **Evidence:** NAO: **32 projects, £196.2bn**; **10 of 32** need urgent action or already cannot be delivered on time; average **12 months late to IOC and 26 months late to FOC**; *"The MoD does not have the information it needs to hold teams to account and make strategic decisions."* — https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/defence-capabilities-delivering-what-was-promised/ . GAO: delivery time *"increased this year to over 12 years"*, 104 programmes, **$2.4trn** — https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108457 . ANAO 2024–25 Major Projects Report: *"The sum of individual project slippage across the 21 projects in the 2024–25 MPR is 404 months"* across projects worth **A$81.5bn** [SEARCH-EXTRACT; page returned 503] — https://www.anao.gov.au/work/major-projects-report/2024-25-major-projects-report
- **Solved today by:** Excel and PowerPoint packs assembled by programme support offices; Primavera P6 / MS Project for schedule; occasionally Jira for software lines; big-4 "assurance reviews" at £1–5k/day. No single-source-of-truth across cost, schedule, technical maturity and supplier performance.
- **Money at stake:** UK alone: a portfolio of £196.2bn where a third is in serious trouble. The GAO-tracked US MDAP portfolio absorbed **$49.3bn of cost growth in one year across 30 programmes** ([GAO-25-107569](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107569)).
- **Software plausible?** **Partly.** The data exists but is politically contested — the blocker is incentive, not technology. A product that reduces the *cost of honest reporting* (auto-ingest from supplier schedules, earned value, and DEFFORM/CDRL submissions) is viable; a product that merely visualises what SROs already hide is not. Medium confidence.

### PP-02 — Inventory and stock data quality on 40-year-old systems

- **Pain:** Defence cannot answer "what do we have, where is it, is it serviceable, and does it still work with the platform?" Stock is simultaneously over-held and unavailable at the front line.
- **Owner:** Defence Equipment & Support (DE&S) inventory/logistics directorates; DLA in the US; national materiel commands; Chief of Materiel.
- **Evidence:** NAO: *"The MoD manages an £11.8 billion portfolio of inventory"*; **640,000 types and more than 740 million individual items**; *"two 40-year-old core inventory systems"*; *"more than 105,500m³ of items stored in central warehouses are not currently fit for use"*; a **£2.5bn pan-defence transformation programme** is under way — https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/defence-inventory-management/ . The Public Accounts Committee found fragmentation the MoD lacks *"the powers needed to deal with"*, and ~**25% staffing gaps** in the relevant programmes [SEARCH-EXTRACT; parliament.uk blocked automated fetch] — https://committees.parliament.uk/work/7919/improving-defence-inventory-management/
- **Solved today by:** Legacy MoD systems (MJDI, CRISP), SAP ECC variants, Team Leidos under the Logistics Commodities & Services Transformation contract, and — universally — local Excel workarounds by unit quartermasters.
- **Money at stake:** £11.8bn of held inventory; historically **£4.2bn of non-explosive inventory had not moved at all for at least two years** (2012 NAO baseline) [SEARCH-EXTRACT]. The £2.5bn transformation programme is itself a budget line.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes, but crowded at the ERP layer.** The whitespace is not another WMS — it is a **reconciliation and data-quality layer** that sits above MJDI/SAP/Leidos and tells a Chief of Materiel where the data is wrong. High confidence on need; note incumbents SAP, Oracle, Leidos, and the MoD's own transformation programme.

### PP-03 — Financial and asset accountability / auditability

- **Pain:** The largest defence organisation on earth cannot produce auditable financial statements, principally because it cannot substantiate the existence, completeness and valuation of property, plant, equipment and inventory.
- **Owner:** DoW Comptroller; Service financial management deputies; DoW OIG; equivalent finance directors in allied MoDs facing NAO/Cour des comptes/Bundesrechnungshof scrutiny.
- **Evidence:** *"DOD received a disclaimer of opinion for the eighth time"*, covering *"$4.65 trillion in reported assets and $4.73 trillion in reported liabilities"*; *"The entities receiving disclaimers of opinion combined accounted for 43% of DOD's total assets and at least 64% of DOD's total budgetary resources"*; statutory deadline for a clean opinion is **31 December 2028** — https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12627.html . DoW OIG reported **26 material weaknesses and 2 significant deficiencies** for FY2025 [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/4365178/independent-auditors-reports-on-the-dod-fy-2025-financial-statements/
- **Solved today by:** Armies of IPA auditors (Ernst & Young, KPMG, Kearney & Company), Audit Remediation Managers, and bespoke "Audit Readiness" consultancies. Remediation is tracked in spreadsheets and NFR (Notice of Findings and Recommendations) trackers.
- **Money at stake:** A hard statutory deadline in 2028 against $4.65trn of assets. The annual DoD audit cost has historically run in the high hundreds of millions [UNVERIFIED — exact FY2025 figure not confirmed].
- **Software plausible?** **Yes — narrowly.** Full ERP remediation is a systems-integrator job. But **NFR/material-weakness remediation workflow, evidence capture and existence-and-completeness sampling** is a genuine SaaS shape with a legislated deadline. High confidence.

### PP-04 — Sub-tier supply chain visibility

- **Pain:** Primes and governments cannot see below tier 1–2. Prohibited content, single points of failure and adversarial ownership sit in tiers 3–5 where no contractual relationship exists.
- **Owner:** Prime contractor supply chain risk directors; OUSD(A&S) Industrial Base Policy; DE&S supply chain resilience teams; programme offices.
- **Evidence:** *"the department does not know who provides parts for suppliers below a certain supplier threshold"*; *"prime contractors have lost some visibility into the sub-tiers of their supply chains, especially below third-tier levels"*; illustratively, in a Honda mapping, *"over 97 percent of its suppliers were below tier one"*; and *"every one of the more than 825 F-35[s] … delivered contains a component made with a Chinese alloy that is prohibited by both U.S. law and Pentagon regulations"* — https://warontherocks.com/in-the-dark-how-the-pentagons-limited-supplier-visibility-risks-u-s-national-security/
- **Solved today by:** **Govini Ark, Exiger, Interos** — all three on the **$919m, decade-long GSA SCRIPTS BPA** sponsored by OUSD(A&S) (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/govini-selected-for-919-million-supply-chain-risk-illumination-contract-vehicle-from-general-services-administration-302422014.html); plus Sourcemap, Z2Data, and supplier surveys in Excel.
- **Money at stake:** $919m of contract vehicle on the US government side alone.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes — but this is the single most contested space in the report.** Treat US-federal "supply chain illumination" as **taken**. The unserved edge is (a) European/NATO equivalents where no SCRIPTS exists, (b) *prime-to-subcontractor data collection workflow* (the actual bottleneck is getting tier-2 to answer, not analysing data once held), and (c) SME-affordable self-attestation. **Sceptical on the core; positive on the edges.**

### PP-05 — Offset / industrial participation obligation management

- **Pain:** Exporters carry multi-decade, multi-country obligations to place work, transfer technology and create jobs in buyer nations, with banked credits, multipliers, milestone deadlines and liquidated-damages exposure. It is tracked in spreadsheets by 1–3 people per prime.
- **Owner:** Head of Offsets / Industrial Participation at a prime or mid-tier exporter; buyer-nation offset authorities (e.g. India's **Defence Offset Management Wing**, https://domw.gov.in/); from 2026, the **UK National Armaments Director**.
- **Evidence:** The UK launched a consultation on **23 October 2025** on a *"Back British"* offsets regime under the Defence Industrial Strategy, closing 23 December 2025, covering direct and indirect offsets — https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/uk-gov-launches-consultation-on-an-offsets-regime-for-the-defence-sector-time-to-102lvvx and https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/uk-consultation-back-british-defence-offset-plan.html . Minister Luke Pollard reconfirmed intent to adopt an offset policy at DPRTE on **25 March 2026** [SEARCH-EXTRACT]. India has imposed penalties of **$38.19m** on defaulting foreign firms [SEARCH-EXTRACT]. A commonly cited industry figure is **$229bn of offset obligations to be discharged globally** — this traces to a 2013-era industry paper and should be treated as **[UNVERIFIED / dated]**.
- **Solved today by:** Excel; PwC India's bespoke *"web-based online application to help OEMs automate their offset compliance monitoring"* (https://www.pwc.in/industries/aerospace-and-defence/defence-offsets-management.html); specialist brokers such as Blenheim Capital Services; in-house tools at Rheinmetall (https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/company/compliance/offset-agreements).
- **Money at stake:** Offset obligations typically run at 30–100% of contract value in the countries that impose them; liquidated damages are commonly 5–10% of the unfulfilled portion [UNVERIFIED as a global average]. The *addressable* spend is compliance headcount and penalty avoidance at perhaps 200–400 exporting firms worldwide.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes — this is the strongest pure-whitespace item in the report.** Obligations are structured data (credits, multipliers, milestones, counterparties, evidence packs). There is no recognised category-defining SaaS incumbent. A brand-new UK regime creates a forced buying moment in 2026–27. **High confidence.**

### PP-06 — Defence cyber certification burden (CMMC, Def Stan 05-138 / DCC, NIS2)

- **Pain:** Thousands of suppliers must evidence controls against frameworks that change under them, annually, per contract, with no reuse across customers or jurisdictions.
- **Owner:** SME owner/MD; Facility Security Officer; IT manager at a 20–500 person supplier; prime supplier-assurance teams who must chase them.
- **Evidence:** **US** — CMMC Phase 2 suspended 13 July 2026 by memorandum from the Under Secretary of War for Acquisition and Sustainment, citing *"excessive costs and administrative burdens, especially on small, medium-sized and non-traditional contractors"*, *"severe shortages in C3PAO capacity"*, and evidence of *"innovative companies to leave or avoid the defense industrial base"*; Phase 1 self-assessment, SPRS scoring, annual affirmations and DFARS 252.204-7012 / NIST SP 800-171 obligations **all remain in force** — https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260720-pentagon-suspends-cmmc-phase-2-requirements-and-launches-review-of-cybersecurity-certification-program . DoD's own 32 CFR Part 170 rule estimated a small entity would spend **$104,670 over three years** on Level 2 certification, excluding implementation and remediation [SEARCH-EXTRACT]. **UK** — Def Stan 05-138 Issue 4 (https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60dc934a8fa8f50abc106c0a/DefStan_05-138_issue_3.pdf is Issue 3; Issue 4 is current) is now evidenced through **Defence Cyber Certification**, with MoD asking **all industry partners to hold DCC Level 0 by 31 December 2026**, across **four levels rising from 3 controls at Level 0 to 144 at Level 3** [SEARCH-EXTRACT].
- **Solved today by:** Managed service providers and CMMC consultancies (Summit 7, PreVeil, Coalfire Federal, Exostar), IASME-licensed certification bodies in the UK, and Word-document SAQs uploaded to MoD's Supplier Cyber Protection Service.
- **Money at stake:** If ~$100k/small entity over three years is even directionally right and tens of thousands of entities are in scope, this is a multi-billion-dollar compliance spend — which is precisely why the Pentagon paused it.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes, but crowded in the US and time-boxed by policy risk.** The CMMC Reform Task Force reported around 11 September 2026; the framework may be materially rewritten. **The better play is the UK/EU side**: DCC Level 0 by 31/12/2026 with "dozens, not hundreds" of certification bodies against a supplier base "in the thousands" is an acute, dated, unserved bottleneck. Medium-high confidence, with explicit policy risk.

### PP-07 — Export control licensing, classification and jurisdiction determination

- **Pain:** Every technical data transfer, foreign national touch, and shipment needs a jurisdiction/classification call and often a licence. Timelines are slipping against target, and the rules are actively changing.
- **Owner:** Export control / trade compliance manager; engineering programme managers who create the technical data; SME MDs who have no compliance function at all.
- **Evidence:** UK DBT: *"In 2025 Q1, 48% of SIEL applications were completed within 20 working days, down from 58% in 2024 Q4"* against a **70% target**; **2,867 SIEL decisions**, of which **2,723 (95%) issued, 140 (5%) refused**; median **22 days** — https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/strategic-export-controls-licensing-statistics-1-january-to-31-march-2025/strategic-export-controls-commentary-1-january-to-31-march-2025 . US: DDTC receives roughly **38,000 licence applications a year from ~14,000 registered entities** [SEARCH-EXTRACT]; ITAR civil penalties reach **$1,267,619 per violation** [SEARCH-EXTRACT]; DDTC has signalled **three further USML revisions in 2026** (space, semiconductors/PCBs, and a redefinition of "defense services"). The **AUKUS ITAR exemption (ITAR §126.7 / §126.15)** was finalised in a rule published in late 2025/January 2026 — https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/department-of-state-publishes-final-rule-finalizing-aukus-defense-trade-itar-exemption
- **Solved today by:** **Descartes Visual Compliance, SAP GTS, E2open, Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE** at the enterprise tier; specialist law firms and ITAR consultants for classification; Excel logs and email at SME tier.
- **Money at stake:** Enterprise global trade management is a mature, high-value software category; the underserved segment is the tens of thousands of sub-£50m-revenue suppliers who currently buy nothing.
- **Software plausible?** **Partly — avoid the enterprise GTM centre.** Whitespace is (a) **AUKUS-specific authorised-user / §126.15 eligibility and record-keeping**, brand new and unserved, and (b) SME-priced classification-and-licence workflow. Medium confidence; strong incumbents.

### PP-08 — Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence (FOCI) and beneficial ownership disclosure

- **Pain:** A proposed DFARS rule pushes FOCI disclosure and mitigation out from the cleared-facility population into the general contractor base, including firms that have never filed an SF 328.
- **Owner:** Contracts and legal leads at defence suppliers; Facility Security Officers; private-equity and VC owners of defence assets; DCSA.
- **Evidence:** *"DOD estimates that more than 37,000 entities—including over 21,000 small businesses"* may be impacted; applies to *"DOD contracts and subcontracts exceeding $5 million"*; requires SF 328 filing, *"contact information for all foreign beneficial owners"*, updates to DCSA throughout performance, and a mitigation strategy *"within 90 days of award, modification, or identification of the risk"*; comments were due 6 July 2026 — https://www.pilieromazza.com/the-shifting-landscape-for-foreign-ownership-control-or-influence-part-1-understanding-the-proposed-rule/ ; Federal Register: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/07/2026-09067/defense-federal-acquisition-regulation-supplement-mitigating-risks-related-to-foreign-ownership
- **Solved today by:** National security law firms (Wiley, Squire Patton Boggs, Wilson Sonsini) at $700–1,200/hour; SF 328 completed in PDF; ownership structures reconstructed manually from cap tables.
- **Money at stake:** 37,000 entities × recurring legal review. Even at a conservative $5–15k/entity/year of advisory and administrative cost, this is a **$200m–500m/yr** annualised burden appearing essentially from nothing.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes.** This is structured cap-table and corporate-genealogy data mapped to a standard form and a 90-day clock. There is no incumbent. **High confidence — but conditional on the rule being finalised; it was still at proposed-rule stage as of mid-2026.**

### PP-09 — Obsolescence / DMSMS across 30-year platforms

- **Pain:** Components live 3–7 years; platforms live 30–50. Last-time-buys, form-fit-function replacements, redesign and requalification consume sustainment budgets unpredictably.
- **Owner:** Obsolescence manager / DMSMS lead in a programme office or prime sustainment organisation; ILS managers.
- **Evidence:** DoD Instruction 4245.15 mandates DMSMS management (https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/424515p.PDF), supported by the **SD-22 guidebook** (January 2026 edition). Market research puts the defence electronics obsolescence market at **$2.7bn (2024) growing to ~$6bn by 2034 at 8.4% CAGR**, of which the **component lifetime / last-time-buy segment was $779.2m in 2024** — vendor-sponsored research, treat as indicative: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/07/08/3111985/28124/en/Defense-Electronics-Obsolescence-Market-Analysis-Report-2025-2034-Structural-Modernization-and-Legacy-Systems-Drive-Demand-Airborne-Platform-Segment-Spurs-Investments-in-Avionics-a.html
- **Solved today by:** **SiliconExpert, Z2Data, Accuris BOM Intelligence** (the latter claims *"1.3 billion components"* and *"~98% verified accuracy"*), plus PCN/EOL alert feeds and in-house Excel BOM risk registers.
- **Money at stake:** ~$2.7bn/yr market today.
- **Software plausible?** **Already solved for electronic components — do not build here.** Named incumbents are strong and data-moated. The residual gaps: **non-electronic obsolescence** (castings, forgings, elastomers, specialty alloys, textiles), **supplier-going-out-of-business risk** rather than part EOL, and the **financial planning of last-time-buys** (how much capital to tie up, for how long, against which forecast). **Low confidence on the core; medium on the adjacencies.**

### PP-10 — Counterfeit and nonconforming parts

- **Pain:** DFARS 252.246-7007/-7008 require a risk-based detection and avoidance system, GIDEP monitoring and reporting, traceability to OCM, and quarantine — implemented as a manual quality process.
- **Owner:** Supplier quality assurance managers; counterfeit-avoidance leads; GIDEP representatives.
- **Evidence:** DFARS 252.246-7007 text: https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.246-7007-contractor-counterfeit-electronic-part-detection-and-avoidance-system. ; a 2022 DOJ case involved *"selling approximately $1 billion of Chinese-made counterfeit Cisco components"* including to DoD — https://warontherocks.com/in-the-dark-how-the-pentagons-limited-supplier-visibility-risks-u-s-national-security/
- **Solved today by:** AS5553/AS6081 processes, independent test labs, GIDEP portal (a 1960s-era government data exchange), and QMS modules in enterprise quality systems.
- **Money at stake:** Difficult to size; the $1bn DOJ case is the largest single published data point.
- **Software plausible?** **Marginal as a standalone.** Best as a feature inside a broader supply-chain provenance product, not a company.

### PP-11 — Munitions ramp-up: capacity, energetics and long-lead planning

- **Pain:** Orders are arriving faster than the industrial base can convert them, and the constraints are several tiers down in chemicals, nozzles, tooling and skilled labour — none of which primes or ministries can model.
- **Owner:** Munitions programme executive offices; national armaments directors; prime operations directors; EU DG DEFIS and the ASAP/EDIP programme teams.
- **Evidence:** FY27 US request: **$70.5bn** for missiles (**Army $36.6bn / Navy $22.6bn / Air Force $11.3bn**) vs **$24.4bn in FY26** — a **188% increase**; PAC-3 MSE **357 → 3,203**; THAAD **55 → 857**; Tomahawk **55 → 785 (+1,327%)**; AMRAAM **464 → 1,811**; quoted constraint: *"For munitions, I think we're going to see solid rocket motors and seekers continue to be really significant bottlenecks"* — https://breakingdefense.com/2026/04/the-pentagon-wants-a-188-percent-bump-for-missile-procurement-can-industry-deliver/ . Europe: ASAP targets **~2 million 155mm shells/year**, against roughly **580,000/year capacity as of mid-2024**; EDIP provides **€1.5bn in grants for 2025–2027** [SEARCH-EXTRACT]. Energetics fragility: an **explosion at Accurate Energetic Systems on 10 October 2025 killed sixteen**; Nammo discovered a sole-source propellant chemical supplier going out of business with no alternative [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://breakingdefense.com/2026/06/dwindling-stockpiles-of-solid-rocket-motors-highlight-industrial-base-challenges/
- **Solved today by:** Bespoke Excel capacity models, supplier phone calls, and consultancy "industrial base assessments" (Avascent, McKinsey, RAND).
- **Money at stake:** $70.5bn/yr of US munitions procurement plus €1.5bn EDIP plus national ammunition programmes.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes — this is the second-strongest opportunity.** A **multi-tier capacity and constraint model** (who can make how much of what, by when, constrained by which shared sub-tier input) is a data-and-modelling product, not an ERP. Nobody owns it: Govini/Exiger illuminate *risk and ownership*, not *throughput*. **High confidence.**

### PP-12 — Sustainment and spares availability (readiness economics)

- **Pain:** Fleets are grounded by parts, not by money. Demand forecasting, depot capacity and supplier lead time are managed in disconnected systems.
- **Owner:** Programme sustainment leads (e.g. the F-35 Joint Program Office); fleet materiel managers; depot commanders.
- **Evidence:** GAO: F-35 mission-capable rate *"declined from 67 percent to 44 percent"* and full-mission-capable *"declined from 38 percent to 25 percent"* between FY2021 and FY2025 while the fleet grew *"from about 450 F-35s in fiscal year 2021 to more than 800 in fiscal year 2025"*; the **Global Support Solution (GSS) Reset** needs *"about $13.7 billion in additional funding from fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2031"* to reach **80% MC / 65% FMC by 2030**; FY2026 need ~**$9.8bn against ~$6.7bn approved**; a Lockheed study found **48 parts with a supplier capacity gap** — https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108113/index.html
- **Solved today by:** Performance-based logistics contracts, ALIS/ODIN for F-35, SAP/IFS/Ramco MRO systems, and Excel supply-demand reconciliations.
- **Money at stake:** $13.7bn on one platform's readiness reset alone.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes, but heavily incumbented** (IFS, SAP, Ramco, Lockheed's ODIN, Boeing, Palantir at DoD). Enter only with a specific wedge such as **cross-fleet spares pooling** or **supplier-capacity-constrained forecasting**. Medium confidence.

### PP-13 — Critical raw materials and single-point-of-failure dependency

- **Pain:** Rare earths, magnets and energetic chemicals concentrate in a single adversary jurisdiction, and export licensing there is a weapon.
- **Owner:** Industrial base policy offices; prime procurement directors; national stockpile agencies.
- **Evidence:** China *"accounts for '63 percent of the world's rare earth mining, 85 percent of rare earth processing, and 92 percent of rare earth magnet production'"* ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/in-the-dark-how-the-pentagons-limited-supplier-visibility-risks-u-s-national-security/)). April 2025 PRC export controls on seven rare earth elements triggered a licensing chokepoint; on 10 July 2025 **DoD took a $400m equity stake in MP Materials**, becoming its largest shareholder, with a **10-year $110/kg NdPr price floor**, a **$150m Office of Strategic Capital loan**, and a **10-year offtake of 100% of the facility's magnet output** — https://fas.org/publication/unpacking-dod-and-mp-partnership/ and https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-new-rare-earth-and-magnet-restrictions-threaten-us-defense-supply-chains
- **Solved today by:** Government equity and offtake deals; Exiger/Govini for exposure mapping; think-tank reports.
- **Money at stake:** $400m equity + $150m loan on one company; wider stockpiling programmes.
- **Software plausible?** **Partly.** Material-level exposure tracing (from BOM → alloy → mine) is real but is being absorbed by SCRIPTS incumbents. Low-medium confidence as a standalone.

### PP-14 — Personnel and facility security clearance throughput

- **Pain:** Cleared headcount is the rate-limiter on winning and delivering classified work; the process is opaque to the employer.
- **Owner:** Facility Security Officers; talent acquisition in cleared businesses; DCSA.
- **Evidence:** As of Q3 FY2025 the average end-to-end background investigation was **243 days (19 to initiate, 215 to investigate, 9 to adjudicate)**; facility clearances typically **45–90 days**, complex cases up to a year; DCSA cut its initial-investigation backlog to ~**100,000 cases by January 2026, down 65%** from the start of 2025 [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2025/05/dcsa-backlog-of-security-clearance-investigations-down-24/ ; DCSA restarted some industry clearances effective 3 November 2025 — https://www.dcsa.mil/About-Us/News/Article/Article/4320068/update-nov-3-2025-some-industry-personnel-and-security-clearances-to-resume/
- **Solved today by:** DISS/NBIS government systems, FSO spreadsheets, and staffing agencies that arbitrage cleared talent.
- **Money at stake:** Cleared-staff wage premiums and unbillable bench time across the DIB; the clearance-staffing industry is substantial [UNVERIFIED size].
- **Software plausible?** **Partly** — the authoritative systems are government-owned and closed. A workforce-planning/forecasting layer for FSOs is plausible but small. Low-medium confidence.

### PP-15 — NATO codification (NSN/NCAGE) and STANAG conformance data

- **Pain:** Selling into NATO nations requires codification of every item — NCAGE registration, technical data packs, NSN assignment — plus STANAG conformance evidence. It is paperwork most SMEs have never done.
- **Owner:** SME commercial/quality managers; National Codification Bureaux; NSPA.
- **Evidence:** UK NCB: commercial codification costs **£65.00 + VAT per item up to 250 items**; routine codification takes **up to 20 working days** with adequate source data; obligations flow from **STANAG 4177** [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/910401/Frequently_Asked_Codification_Questions.pdf . NSPA manages **41 Support Partnerships** and, via **32 multinational support partnerships, over 90 major weapon systems**; the NATO Logistics Stock Exchange generates *"a yearly turnover of more than 200 MEUR"* [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://www.nspa.nato.int/about/support-to-operations/nlse
- **Solved today by:** National Codification Bureau portals, NSPA ePortal, a handful of codification bureaux/consultancies, and Excel.
- **Money at stake:** Modest per item, large in aggregate as the supplier base expands under NATO 3.5%; mostly an *access* cost rather than a waste.
- **Software plausible?** **Yes, as a component of an SME onboarding product**, not as a standalone. Medium confidence.

### PP-16 — SME access to defence work

- **Pain:** SMEs cannot find, qualify for, or afford to bid defence work; frameworks lock them out; primes do not flow opportunity down.
- **Owner:** SME founders/MDs; MoD Defence Office for Small Business Growth; DoW Office of Small Business Programs; prime supplier development teams.
- **Evidence:** *"In 2024, just 5% of the Ministry of Defence's direct procurement spend went to SMEs"*; *"the top five suppliers alone accounted for over 29% of all MOD procurement spending"* in 2025; *"In 2025, 10 framework agreements…were used to award more than £6bn worth of new MOD contracts. Of these top 10 frameworks, half contained fewer than 20 suppliers, and three contained fewer than ten"* — https://www.tussell.com/insights/defence-procurement-tracker . Policy response: **+£2.5bn SME spend by summer 2028 (a 50% increase)** against current SME spend of **~£5bn/yr of which only 25% is direct award** — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mod-small-and-medium-sized-enterprise-sme-action-plan/ministry-of-defences-small-and-medium-sized-enterprise-sme-action-plan . US: the number of small businesses in the DIB *"shrunk by over 40%"* over a decade per DoD's State of Competition report [UNVERIFIED — source page returned 503]; DoD vendors reportedly fell from **~56,000 to ~41,000** [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2024/09/a-closer-look-at-the-shrinking-defense-industrial-base/
- **Solved today by:** Tender aggregators (**DCI/Supply2Defence, Stotles, Tussell, Deltek GovWin, DefenceLens**), bid-writing consultancies at 2–5% of contract value, and trade bodies (ADS, Make UK Defence).
- **Money at stake:** £2.5bn of newly targeted UK SME spend; equivalent political targets in the US, Germany and the EU.
- **Software plausible?** **Tender discovery is taken** (named above). The unserved problem is **everything after discovery**: becoming *biddable* — DCC/CMMC status, codification, export control posture, FOCI disclosure, quality approvals, cyber posture, and the reusable evidence pack behind them. **High confidence on the "supplier readiness passport" framing; low confidence on another tender feed.**

### PP-17 — Collaborative and joint procurement coordination

- **Pain:** Europe has money but buys nationally; joint programmes are coordination-poor across requirements, schedules, configurations and payment.
- **Owner:** EDA; national armaments directors; EU DG DEFIS; SAFE beneficiary ministries.
- **Evidence:** *"Collaborative procurement represented 24% of total equipment spending"* against an EDA benchmark of 35%, on **€115bn of equipment procurement** in 2025 — https://defence-industry.eu/eu-defence-spending-jumps-20-to-e418-billion-in-2025-with-budgets-projected-to-approach-e550-billion-by-2029/ . SAFE provides **€150bn**, subscribed by **19 Member States**, with a first wave of **~€38bn approved for 8 Member States in January 2026**, and requires common procurement *"involving at least two participating countries"* — https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/safe/ and https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/commission-approves-first-wave-defence-funding-eight-member-states-under-safe-2026-01-15_en
- **Solved today by:** OCCAR and NSPA programme offices, EDA project teams, and email.
- **Money at stake:** The gap between 24% and 35% of €115bn is roughly **€12.6bn/yr of procurement not being done collaboratively**.
- **Software plausible?** **Weakly.** The barrier is sovereignty and industrial politics, not tooling. **Low confidence — flagged as a trap.**

### PP-18 — Foreign Military Sales case management and delivery visibility

- **Pain:** Partner nations buy through FMS and then wait years with poor visibility of case status, delivery schedules and offsets.
- **Owner:** Partner-nation defence attachés and procurement agencies; DSCA; US primes managing FMS cases.
- **Evidence:** FY2025 FMS-system total was **$104.4bn**, including **$75.9bn in partner-funded arms sales**; the FMS programme historically carried **15,365 open cases valued at $620bn across 163 countries** (FY2020 baseline) [SEARCH-EXTRACT] — https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11437 . Reform is live: **EO 14268** and **EO 14383 (6 February 2026)**, and on **10 February 2026 DoD realigned DSCA and DTSA under USD(A&S)** to create a *"single enterprise"* — same source.
- **Solved today by:** DSCA's own systems (DSAMS), embassy liaison, and spreadsheets on the customer side.
- **Money at stake:** A $620bn open-case pipeline.
- **Software plausible?** **Partly.** The authoritative data is US-government-held and not exportable; a buyer-side case-tracking and offset-linked product is plausible but depends on data access. Medium-low confidence.

## 4. Top 8 whitespace opportunities, ranked

**1. Offset / industrial participation obligation management platform.**
Highest-conviction pick. Structured, multi-decade obligations with credits, multipliers, milestones and liquidated damages; no SaaS incumbent; and a **brand-new UK regime being stood up by the National Armaments Director** after the October–December 2025 consultation, creating a forced buying moment. Buyers are identifiable (heads of offsets at ~200–400 exporting firms; buyer-nation offset authorities like India's DOMW). Both sides of the market are addressable, which is rare.
*Risk:* small number of logos; long enterprise sales cycles; the UK regime could still be watered down.

**2. Multi-tier munitions and industrial capacity modelling.**
$70.5bn of FY27 US missile procurement chasing a base that "can't buy that many in a single year," with energetics, seekers and solid rocket motors as named chokepoints, plus ASAP/EDIP in Europe against a 2m-shells/year target. The question "if I place this order, when does it actually arrive, and what shared sub-tier input does it collide with?" is answered today in Excel. Distinct from Govini/Exiger, who illuminate ownership and risk, not throughput.
*Risk:* data acquisition from sub-tier suppliers is the hard part; may require government sponsorship.

**3. Supplier compliance "readiness passport" for the defence supply chain (UK/EU first).**
One evidence graph per supplier — DCC/Def Stan 05-138 level, Cyber Essentials, NIST 800-171 SPRS score, ITAR/EAR posture, FOCI disclosure, NCAGE/NSN codification, quality approvals — reused across every prime and every bid. The forcing function is explicit: **DCC Level 0 for all MoD industry partners by 31 December 2026**, with certification-body capacity "in the dozens" against a supplier base "in the thousands." Directly serves MoD's **+£2.5bn SME spend by 2028** target and the primes who must chase their tier-2s.
*Risk:* must avoid becoming another tender feed; value is in evidence reuse, not discovery.

**4. FOCI / beneficial ownership disclosure and mitigation workflow.**
The proposed DFARS rule reaches **"more than 37,000 entities—including over 21,000 small businesses"** at a $5m threshold, with a 90-day mitigation clock and continuous update obligations to DCSA. Currently a law-firm deliverable. Corporate-ownership genealogy mapped to SF 328 is a clean software problem.
*Risk:* **rule was at proposed stage (comments closed 6 July 2026) and may change or slip.** Rank 4 is contingent on finalisation — verify before committing roadmap.

**5. Audit and material-weakness remediation workflow for defence finance organisations.**
An **eighth consecutive disclaimer** over **$4.65trn of assets**, **26 material weaknesses**, and a **statutory clean-opinion deadline of 31 December 2028** that the department is not on track to meet. Existing spend flows to IPAs and consultancies; the artefact management (NFRs, corrective action plans, evidence, existence-and-completeness sampling) is spreadsheet-based. Allied MoDs facing NAO/Bundesrechnungshof pressure are a second market.
*Risk:* long federal sales cycles; may be perceived as an incumbent-consultancy adjacency.

**6. Inventory data-quality and reconciliation layer above legacy logistics systems.**
£11.8bn of UK inventory, 740m items, two 40-year-old core systems, 105,500m³ unfit for use, and a **£2.5bn transformation programme** already funded. The play is not a new WMS but a read-only truth layer that quantifies and prioritises data defects across MJDI/SAP/Leidos estates — exactly the gap the NAO and PAC describe.
*Risk:* the £2.5bn programme may absorb the need; must position as accelerating it, not competing.

**7. Programme portfolio assurance and evidence automation for national armaments directorates.**
"47 of 49 major projects delayed or over budget", a portfolio 12 months late to IOC and 26 to FOC, GAO's 12-year average, and ANAO's 404 months of cumulative slippage. New National Armaments Director organisations (UK), the Defence Investment Plan's 10-year budgeting shift, and the DIP's **£4.7bn unfunded gap** all create demand for credible, low-friction programme truth.
*Risk:* **the constraint is political incentive, not tooling.** Only viable if it reduces the *cost* of reporting rather than merely exposing bad news. Ranked 7 for that reason.

**8. AUKUS / allied export-control authorisation management.**
The ITAR §126.7/§126.15 exemption framework is new (final rule late 2025/January 2026), with 700+ authorised Australian entities and a redefinition of "defense services" among three planned 2026 USML revisions. Authorised-user status, eligibility determination and record-keeping are novel obligations that Descartes/SAP GTS do not yet cover natively.
*Risk:* narrow; could be absorbed by incumbents within 24 months. Best as a wedge, not a company.

**Explicitly rejected as traps:** component-level obsolescence (SiliconExpert/Z2Data/Accuris own it); US federal supply-chain illumination (SCRIPTS locks in Govini/Exiger/Interos for a decade); defence tender discovery (commoditised); EU collaborative procurement coordination (a sovereignty problem wearing a software costume); enterprise global trade management (Descartes/SAP/E2open).
