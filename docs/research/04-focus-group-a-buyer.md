## 2. The four questions you asked me

### 2.1 "Back British" — what would I require of a UK exporter, and would a UK authority buy software to run it?

Careful with the framing. **Back British is an *import* offset regime.** It requires *overseas* suppliers winning MOD contracts to place work, jobs, skills and investment in the UK. A UK exporter's obligations run the other way — to Saudi GAMI, Tawazun, DOMW, DAPA, SSB — and are unaffected by Back British. Both are UK customers; they are not the same customer.

**What I would require, as the authority.** The published consultation still commits only to *"monitoring mechanisms and enforcement measures to address underperformance"* and *"multipliers to drive investment to priority areas and sub-sectors"* ([GOV.UK, DIS Offset Written Consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/defence-industrial-strategy-dis-offset-written-consultation/defence-industrial-strategy-dis-offset-written-consultation)). It names no threshold, no administering body and no credit methodology. My requirement would be: a signed industrial participation schedule per contract; annual evidenced returns against it; named UK recipient entities with Companies House numbers and region; multiplier justification against declared priority sub-sectors; and a liquidated-damages calculation that survives legal challenge. Note what is *not* there: a banked-credit market. The UK will more likely land on **contractual commitments with milestones** than a tradable credit ledger — if your data model assumes credits-and-banking you have built for India, not for me.

**Would a UK authority buy software to administer it?** Eventually yes, and the procurement would be unusually clean because there is nothing to displace. The owner is the **National Armaments Director Group**, now sitting over DE&S with roughly a £20bn annual budget including an £11bn "Invest" budget, formed by consolidating eight procurement budgets into one ([DE&S](https://des.mod.uk/rupert-pearce-named-new-uk-national-armaments-director/); [DSEI Gateway](https://www.dsei.co.uk/news/uk-selects-national-armament-director-transform-defence-procurement)). But **I cannot buy a system of record for rules that do not exist.** Realistic first money: FY2027/28. Build for the exporter now; be the obvious choice when the authority requirement lands.

### 2.2 The SME visibility gap — would MOD fund closing it, and from where?

**Yes, and this is the most under-rated item on your list.**

The gap is undisputed: ~5% of direct procurement spend reached SMEs in 2024, roughly **75% of MOD's ~£5bn SME spend flows indirectly through primes where MOD cannot see it**, and the commitment is +£2.5bn by May 2028 ([SME Action Plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mod-small-and-medium-sized-enterprise-sme-action-plan/ministry-of-defences-small-and-medium-sized-enterprise-sme-action-plan); [Tussell](https://www.tussell.com/insights/defence-procurement-tracker)).

The killer detail is *how* MOD measures the 75% it cannot see. Make UK Defence has called for an independent audit or batch-test of the primes' annual returns, on the basis that the MOD survey is *"effectively a 'mark your own homework' exercise"* ([Karve International](https://www.karveinternational.com/insights/smes-and-mid-tiers---the-forgotten-element-in-uk-defence-procurement)). **I am publicly committed to a number I calculate from a self-assessed survey by the parties with the incentive to inflate it.** That is the sentence an NAO report gets built around, and I know it.

**Budget line:** the Defence Office for Small Business Growth, stood up January 2026, reaching full operating capability in **December 2026** with detailed direct/indirect targets and accountability mechanisms explicitly planned for FOC. **Who signs:** DOSBG's director for a discovery under ~£100k; above ~£250k it needs a full business case and 9–18 months.

**What I would buy:** not a passport — a **prime subcontract-spend assurance return**: structured submission per strategic supplier, entity-resolved to Companies House so "SME" is verified rather than asserted, with double-count detection across primes and a variance report I can defend. A *measurement assurance* product is the one thing the primes cannot credibly supply, because they are the measured party.

### 2.3 DCC Level 0 by 31 December 2026 — a real forcing function I would spend against?

**It is a real deadline. It is not a real MOD spend line, and one premise in the team's research is wrong.**

The deadline is confirmed at source: Eleanor Fairford, MOD Director of Cyber Defence & Risk — *"I have also recently asked all industry partners to achieve Level 0 DCC certification by 31st December 2026"* ([Defence Digital blog, 8 May 2026](https://defencedigital.blog.gov.uk/2026/05/08/one-year-of-defence-cyber-certification-building-stronger-cyber-resilience-together/)).

**The correction:** research 01 and 02 both rest on certification-body capacity being *"dozens, not hundreds"*. IASME — MOD's official certification partner — **manages a network of over 350 certification bodies across the UK and Crown Dependencies** ([IASME](https://iasme.co.uk/certification-bodies/)). Level 0 is three controls, and the market around it is already crowded (CyberSmart, Periculo, Bridewell, RightCue, Fig, Indelible Data). The bottleneck your thesis depends on is an order of magnitude smaller than assumed. **Re-baseline before anyone builds on that number.**

Would I spend against it? No. Suppliers pay for their own certification — the settled model (JOSCAR: **£725 + VAT/yr** above £1m turnover; DCC: suppliers pay CBs). The one thing MOD might fund is what the blog conspicuously omits: **how MOD tracks who has certified**. I would pay for a status register and nothing else — a few hundred thousand pounds, competing against IASME simply providing the feed free. Thin.

### 2.4 The Segmented Acquisition Model's three months — would I genuinely use it?

**Partly. It is real, but it measures the wrong clock for you.**

The model is live from April 2026 and routes software, AI and off-the-shelf capability through a three-month lane against a previous average of roughly 6.5 years to award contracts above £20m — the "3-2-1" model ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/redesigning-uk-defense-procurement-ai-era)). It is a genuine reform and I will use it.

But the three months is **contracting time from an approved requirement with money against it**. It does not shorten requirement definition, create budget, or survive the Defence Investment Plan's £4.7bn unfunded gap. For a £100k subscription it is also *slower than what already exists*: a **G-Cloud 15 direct award** needs no competition and I can place it in weeks.

**So-what:** stop optimising for the three-month lane. **Get on G-Cloud 15 or accept an 18-month wait** — it was awarded 6 August 2026 and does not reopen to new suppliers for roughly 18 months. That is the single most consequential calendar fact in your plan.

## 3. Per-candidate buy/no-buy, UK budget lines and signatories

### 3.1 Offsets — obligor side (UK exporter with foreign obligations)

**Buy: yes, and this customer can pay this quarter.** The buyer is a head of industrial participation at a UK exporter — BAE, Babcock, Leonardo UK, Thales UK, MBDA UK, QinetiQ, plus mid-tiers — carrying obligations into GAMI (50% localisation of military spend by 2030 against 24.89% at end-2024), Tawazun (60% of contract value, secured by an unconditional 8.5% bank guarantee), DAP 2020 (30%) and DAPA. It is corporate money, no public-sector business case, no Cabinet Office gate. **Who signs:** a commercial or compliance director, typically £50k–£250k without board approval.
**What kills it:** the 1–3 people per prime who currently do this in Excel are also the people who would be displaced, and they own the evaluation.

### 3.2 Offsets — authority side (NAD, Back British)

**Buy: conditional, FY2027/28.** Budget line: NAD Group. **Who signs:** at £60k–£150k on a G-Cloud direct award, a commercial officer with standing delegation; helpfully, most Cabinet Office digital and technology spend controls **ceased as a requirement from 1 April 2026**, removing a gate that used to add three months ([CDDO](https://cddo.blog.gov.uk/2024/05/14/understanding-the-new-digital-and-technology-spend-controls/)).
**What kills it:** the policy not landing; a credits-and-multipliers data model that does not match a commitments-and-milestones regime; and the conflict in §4.

### 3.3 Inventory data-quality layer

**Fundable, displacement-blocked.** £11.8bn of inventory, 640,000 types, 740m items, two 40-year-old core systems, **105,500m³ of central-warehouse stock not currently fit for use** ([NAO](https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/defence-inventory-management/)), against an already-funded £2.5bn transformation programme. Budget line: Defence Support / DE&S; the programme SRO signs.
**What kills it:** the **£6.7bn Leidos LCST contract**, which already covers storage, distribution and the procurement and inventory management of 70,000 commodity NSNs and has just completed a mid-life refresh delivering £272m of savings ([Leidos](https://www.leidos.com/company/global/uk-europe/LCST)). I will be asked: *"why is this not in scope of a contract I already pay £6.7bn for?"* The only answer that works: **because the prime cannot mark its own homework on data quality.**

### 3.4 Readiness passport / DCC

**No-buy.** See §2.3. I already pay for Hellios JOSCAR, IASME's 350-CB network, the Supplier Cyber Protection Service and Commerce Decisions AWARD. A fifth supplier-data system invites the obvious audit question, and the aggregate is a classification problem (§5).

### 3.5 Munitions capacity modelling

**No-buy as SaaS.** The inputs are proprietary line rates and named sub-tier chokepoints; the output is a map of how to stop UK munitions output with a handful of interventions. UK SECRET. The realistic UK procurement is a classified industrial base assessment to a cleared body, not a subscription.

### 3.6 FOCI

**Out of scope.** A US DFARS *proposed* rule, comments closed 6 July 2026, still unfinalised, expanding DCSA's caseload from ~2,000 to ~41,000 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/07/2026-09067/defense-federal-acquisition-regulation-supplement-mitigating-risks-related-to-foreign-ownership)). No UK budget line owns it; the nearest UK analogue sits with the Investment Security Unit under the National Security and Investment Act, not with defence commercial.

## 4. The two-sided question, answered honestly — UK version

**What I need that an obligor-side tool would never give me:** a register authoritative *independently of the obligor*; **cross-obligor aggregation** ("what is the total outstanding industrial participation commitment to the UK, by year, region and sub-sector, across every overseas supplier?" — no contractor tool can ever show me that, and it is exactly what a minister asks); adjudication with a defensible audit trail; **double-counting detection**, where the same UK subcontract is claimed by two overseas primes; and a policy counterfactual, because Canada's Auditor General found the benefits and full costs of its equivalent policy *"were unknown"* ([OAG Report 10](https://www.canada.ca/en/auditor-general/our-work/audit-reports/parl-oag-202412-10-e.html)) and I will be asked the same.

**Would I tolerate the vendor also serving the companies I regulate?**

**Not in the configuration you would naturally build.** If you sell overseas obligors a tool that *optimises* discharge — cheapest qualifying transaction, maximum multiplier capture, claims structured for acceptance — while selling the NAD the tool that *adjudicates* those claims, that is selling the exam and the revision guide. Under the Procurement Act 2023 I must prepare and publish a conflicts assessment before issuing a tender notice, and where a conflict puts a supplier at unfair advantage and cannot be avoided, **exclusion is mandatory** ([Procurement Pathway](https://www.procurementpathway.civilservice.gov.uk/documents/guidance/conflicts-of-interest); [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/contents)).

**The tolerable configuration, stated precisely:** be the pipe, not the adviser. I would require, in the contract: a bar on advisory, brokerage or optimisation services to any obligor with a live UK obligation, for the term plus two years; **no cross-tenant derived product** — no benchmarks, no "market rates for multiplier capture" sold back to industry from data I put in; legally and technically separated tenancy evidenced by an independent **ISAE 3000 / SOC 2 Type II** report scoped to tenant isolation; the authority holding keys and an unconditional export right; written disclosure of every UK-obligated customer within five working days of signature; and audit and step-in rights.

**The UK-specific nuance that helps you:** a UK exporter's obligations run to *foreign* authorities. Serving BAE's Saudi obligations does not conflict with serving the NAD's Back British register, because the NAD does not regulate BAE's GAMI commitments. **That is a defensible separation and it is the one I would accept.** What I would not accept is you also serving the overseas primes bidding into UK contracts.

## 5. Where the UK classification line actually is

UK **OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE is a handling caveat, not a separate accreditation regime** — Kahootz sells OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE collaboration on G-Cloud at £3.69–£11.69 per user per month. The question is not whether you can hold OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE; it is which fields sit above it. And **do not attempt G-Cloud Lot 1b** (above OFFICIAL): £75m insurance plus a Gold Standard Financial Viability Assessment.

| Candidate | Fine at OFFICIAL / OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE | **Never in your cloud** |
|---|---|---|
| **Offsets (obligor)** | Obligation value, ratio, milestones, evidence packs, counterparties, multiplier justification — commercial-in-confidence, not government-classified | Technical data moved under a technology-transfer obligation. That is export-controlled and belongs in the exporter's controlled environment, not your workflow |
| **Offsets (authority)** | Post-award commitments, returns, adjudications, named UK recipient entities | **Pre-announcement programme identity and forward buy intent.** A national offset pipeline is a forward order book. Mitigation: anonymised programme IDs until award publication, no linkage table in the system |
| **SME spend visibility** | Subcontract values, Companies House numbers, region, SME status | Platform-attributed sub-tier supplier maps. Mitigation: aggregate and category reporting only |
| **Readiness passport** | A supplier's own status, shared by consent | **The aggregate.** Thousands of UK suppliers ranked by weakest cyber posture is a prioritised target list. Individually OFFICIAL; collectively above it |
| **Inventory** | **Defect telemetry** — record counts, completeness, duplicate rates, reconciliation variances, age of record | **Holdings.** Quantity and location by NSN, especially guided weapons and ammunition |
| **Munitions capacity** | Published budget lines and announced targets | Everything that makes it useful. SECRET |

**The rule I apply:** individual records are usually OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE; **the aggregate is what gets classified up.** Design the schema so the sensitive join is impossible, not merely disabled — and be able to prove it to a Secure by Design assessor.

## 6. Top three objections that would kill a UK procurement — and the evidence that overcomes each

**Objection 1 — "You will not exist in five years and the obligation runs for fifteen."**
Industrial participation commitments run 10–15 years; vendor failure mid-obligation means losing the audit trail for a live legal exposure.
**Evidence that overcomes it:** escrow with a named agent and a *tested* release, demonstrated live; full ledger export in an open documented schema including evidence binaries; a rehearsed restore proving the register is reconstructible from exports alone; financial standing evidence; contractual step-in. **Show me a successful restore** — that demonstration is worth more than your entire deck.

**Objection 2 — "You also sell to the people I regulate."**
Unmitigated, this is mandatory exclusion, not a conversation. **Evidence:** the §4 clause set — separated tenancy with an ISAE 3000/SOC 2 Type II report scoped to it, no cross-tenant derived product, disclosure of obligated customers, and the UK-specific separation argument (foreign-authority obligations ≠ NAD-regulated obligations) written into my conflicts assessment for me.

**Objection 3 — "This becomes a permanent running cost and the NAO asks why I didn't use what I already have."**
Not hypothetical, and very fresh: Defence Digital built a commercial audit tool to spot MOD overpayments and booked **£2.15m in constructive losses**, the stated reason being that *"maintaining the original tool would have involved ongoing running costs, it was decided that continuing to support it did not represent value for money"* ([PublicTechnology, 17 September 2026](https://www.publictechnology.net/2026/09/17/defence-and-security/mod-records-2m-loss-on-digital-tool-created-to-spot-overpayments/)). That will be quoted at me.
**Evidence that overcomes it:** a quantified status-quo baseline in FTE days; five-year TCO against it; a **G-Cloud call-off with short termination rather than a bespoke build**, so exit is cheap; a benefits realisation plan my finance director will counter-sign; a named UK comparator already running it.

**Honourable mention — "Tell the prime to do it."** My instinct; have an answer ready. Under the Single Source Contract Regulations a prime's compliance tooling is recovered as overhead through the Allowable Costs AAR test ([SSRO](https://www.ssro.gov.uk/allowable-costs-guidance-overheads-and-indirect-costs)) — **I pay either way, just opaquely, and I do not get the data.** The counter that works on me is one sentence: *the regulated party cannot be the system of record for its own compliance.* Note the single-source reporting threshold has just risen from £5m to £25m, so I now see *less*, not more.

## 7. What I would actually sign tomorrow

1. **A 12-month, £60k–£150k G-Cloud 15 direct-award call-off** for an **obligation register** — sold today to a UK exporter, architected so the same schema serves the NAD when Back British lands: OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE, UK-hosted, anonymised programme IDs, full export, escrow, §4 conflicts clauses. **If you are not on G-Cloud 15, you have lost eighteen months.**

2. **A subscription to a date-versioned register of every offset and local-content regime a UK exporter faces** — GAMI/LCGPA, Tawazun, DAP 2020, SSB, DAPA, ITB, AIC, the Polish Offset Act, Greece's 25% guideline, Back British when published — snapshotted daily, diffed, history retained. Unclassified, no conflict problem, no integration, and **impossible to backfill**. Cheapest thing on your roadmap and I would buy it from a vendor I had never met.

3. **A four-week paid DOSBG discovery on indirect SME spend assurance** — take three primes' latest annual returns, entity-resolve against Companies House, tell me the error rate. If it is material you have written my next business case, and I will find the money before DOSBG reaches FOC in December 2026.

**What I would not sign at any price:** the munitions model, the FOCI workflow, or anything putting an aggregate ranking of the UK supplier base on a commercial platform.

**Final word.** Your experts picked offsets for the right reason and, for the UK, the wrong customer. The exporter can pay this quarter; the authority cannot pay until its own policy exists. Build for the exporter, keep the authority schema in the same product, and keep the two commercially separable — because the day I find you advising the overseas primes bidding into my contracts, my conflicts assessment fails and I am **required** to exclude you.
