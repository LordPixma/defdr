# Focus Group B — "The Supplier": an industry-side verdict on the five DEFDR candidates

**Author:** Focus Group Agent B (composite: VP Offsets/Industrial Participation at a tier-1 exporter; bid director at a mid-tier; MD of a 40-person UK defence SME)
**Date:** 19 September 2026
**Audience:** DEFDR product team
**Bias declaration:** I do not care about your architecture. I care about winning bids, cash, headcount, and not personally signing something that gets me sued or debarred. I will pay for software that makes me money or removes a liability from my signature. I will not pay for software that creates work for my team.

---

## 1. Verdict and ranking

Two experts ranked offsets #1 whitespace. From my chair they are **half right and dangerously half wrong**. Offsets is genuinely unserved by software. It is also the single worst dataset in my company to hand to a third party, and the pain is not where you think it is: the pain is *discharging* obligations, which is relationship and deal work, not *tracking* them, which is a spreadsheet nobody would fund a purchase order for.

My ranking, ordered by "would I sign a PO for this inside 90 days":

| # | Candidate | Would I buy? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Offsets — but only the pre-award half** (country rulebook + bid-time exposure model) | **Yes, tomorrow, £25k, on my own delegated authority** | Bought from campaign/B&P budget, no customer data leaves my building, no InfoSec review, directly affects whether I win |
| **2** | **FOCI / beneficial-ownership workflow** | **Yes, £20–40k — the moment the DFARS rule is final** | Best liability shape of all five. SF 328 is certified by a named human. That human will buy insurance. |
| **3** | **Supplier readiness passport** | **Yes at SME tier (£500–£1,500/yr, credit card); reluctantly at prime tier** | Real deadline (DCC Level 0, 31 Dec 2026), thousands of buyers, but four entrenched incumbents and the lowest ACV |
| **4** | **Offsets — the post-award obligation ledger** | **No. Not in year one. Possibly never on shared infrastructure.** | See §4. This is the idea-killer section. |
| **5** | **Multi-tier munitions capacity modelling** | **No** | Right pain, wrong buyer, data I cannot legally or practically give you |
| **6** | **Inventory data-quality layer** | **Not my budget at all** | This is DE&S's and DLA's problem. No industry buyer exists. Good government product; zero relevance to me. |

The headline: **offsets is a real whitespace and a bad first product. Split it. Sell me the rulebook before you ask for my ledger.**

## 2. Who has the budget and the pain — per candidate

The question that matters is not "who feels the pain" but "whose signature is on the line, and what cost centre do they already spend from." Software that needs a new budget line dies. Software that displaces an existing services line gets bought.

### 2.1 Offsets / industrial participation

- **Signs the PO:** VP or Director, Industrial Participation & Offsets. Real titles: "Director, Industrial Participation" (Lockheed-type), "Head of Industrial Participation" (BAE-type), "Head of Offset Management" — Rheinmetall runs it inside Compliance ([Rheinmetall](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/company/compliance/offset-agreements)). Above ~£50k it escalates to the Group Contracts Director or sector CFO.
- **Budget:** *campaign / bid & proposal* at pre-award; *programme overhead* post-award, frequently charged to the contract as an allowable cost. A minority run it from Corporate Compliance. Almost never IT.
- **Today's spend it displaces:** consultants. NyRad, PwC India, CORE (UAE), MMC, Global Trade Solutions, Corvitex, Cranfield Defence Management Lab, and the Oliver Wyman/Avascent tier. This is the key fact: **there is money on the table, it just currently has a consultant's name on the invoice.**
- **Personal liability: medium-to-high, but indirect.** The offset compliance certification to an authority is signed by a named officer. The UAE Tawazun regime requires an **unconditional, on-demand bank guarantee equal to 8.5% of the offset obligation**, callable on a missed milestone ([BSA](https://www.bsalaw.com/insight/warning-to-contractors-dealing-with-gcc-governments/)). India levies **5% of the unfulfilled obligation** ([PRS summary of the 22 July 2026 report](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/management-of-defence-offsets)). A called guarantee is a board-level event and a career-ending one for the VP. **But note the asymmetry:** the liability lands on *failure to discharge*, not on *failure to track*. Software that only tracks does not move my liability.

### 2.2 Supplier compliance "readiness passport"

- **Signs the PO:** at a 40-person SME, the **Managing Director**, personally, on a company card. At a prime, the **Supply Chain Assurance Manager** or **Head of Supplier Quality**, out of procurement opex, at £50–150k.
- **Personal liability:** low for the prime, real for the SME MD — no DCC Level 0 by **31 December 2026** and I lose the right to bid. That is not liability, that is revenue. Revenue arguments sell faster than liability arguments to SMEs.
- **Nice-to-have or must-have?** Must-have with a date on it, but **the most contested space of the five**. Incumbents: Hellios/JOSCAR (the de facto UK answer, £725+VAT/yr, free under £1m turnover), Exostar, Achilles, Avetta. Their pricing is all bespoke-quoted ([Exostar](https://www.exostar.com/products/supplier-management/); [Achilles via Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/achilles-platform); [Avetta](https://www.avetta.com/suppliers-contractors)). Selling me a *second* passport is selling me more work. Selling me the *preparation* for the passport I already have to get is selling me time back.

### 2.3 FOCI / beneficial ownership

- **Signs the PO:** General Counsel, VP Contracts, or the Facility Security Officer. Budget: legal & compliance — the easiest budget in the building to raid, because it already funds $700–1,200/hour outside counsel.
- **Personal liability: the highest of any candidate, and it is the right kind.** SF 328 is certified by a named individual and filed into NISS, with beneficial-ownership disclosure kept current throughout performance and mitigation implemented **within 90 calendar days** ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/05/proposed-rule-issued-to-implement-expanded-foci-disclosure-and-risk-mitigation-requirements-for-defense-contractors); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/07/2026-09067/defense-federal-acquisition-regulation-supplement-mitigating-risks-related-to-foreign-ownership)). A false certification is a personal exposure, not a corporate one. **People buy software to protect their own signature. This is the textbook case.**
- **Incumbents in software:** none found. Confirmed across multiple searches — the entire market is law firms and PDFs.
- **The catch:** it was still a *proposed* rule with comments closed 6 July 2026. I will not buy against a proposed rule. I will buy the week it is final.

### 2.4 Multi-tier munitions capacity modelling

- **Signs the PO:** VP Operations or Director of Industrial Strategy at a prime; realistically, a programme executive office on the government side.
- **Budget:** programme overhead or a capex business case. Both are slow and both require a CFO.
- **Personal liability: none.** This is a planning nice-to-have. My ops director already knows his bottleneck is castings, energetics and nozzles — Breaking Defense has printed it, NATO has printed it. He does not need a model telling him he is short of solid rocket motors. He needs castings.
- **Killer objection:** the product only works if tier-3 and tier-4 suppliers disclose capacity. They will not, to me, let alone to you. Capacity disclosure is pricing power. This needs government compulsion to exist, which means a government sale, which means the 10x-slower path.

### 2.5 Inventory data-quality layer

- **There is no industry buyer.** The pain is DE&S's and DLA's. Every pound of value accrues to the customer. I would not spend £1 on it, and if MOD asked me to adopt it, I would ask who is paying for my integration effort. Excellent product; wrong focus group.

**Personal-liability purchases (the good kind), ranked:** FOCI ≫ offsets ≫ readiness passport ≫ munitions capacity ≈ inventory (neither).

## 3. Offsets: why has no SaaS won? The honest answer, with names

I searched for an offset management SaaS the way a buyer would. Here is what actually exists.

**Named incumbents and near-incumbents — all real, none a category-defining SaaS:**

| Name | What it actually is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **PwC India** — offset compliance monitoring application | A **bespoke consultancy-delivered web app** that auto-generates periodic offset compliance reports, offset proposal documents and banking-offer documents ([PwC](https://www.pwc.in/industries/aerospace-and-defence/defence-offsets-management.html)) | Real product, sold as a consulting wrapper, India-scoped. The closest thing to an incumbent. |
| **NyRad + Contextere — "offset co-pilot"** | Canada's leading ITB/offset consultancy partnered with an AI firm (Madison platform) in **May 2023** to build "a smart ITB management tool" ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/TheNewswire.com/17329306/nyrad-announces-partnership-with-contextere-to-develop-an-ai-powered-itb-management-tool/); [NyRad](https://nyrad.ca/perspectives/announcement-nyrad-announces-partnership-with-contextere-to-develop-an-ai-powered-itb-management-tool)) | **This is your nearest competitor and you should assume it exists.** Three years on I found no evidence of a launched, generally-available product — which is itself the most useful data point in this document. A consultancy with $5bn of obligations under management tried to productise and it has not shipped visibly. |
| **Epicos** | Subscription B2B portal: 3,500-entity industry directory, ~90-country intelligence, and the **"Project Opportunities"** service for identifying and structuring offset/IC projects ([Epicos](https://www.epicos.com/)) | A real, funded, recurring-revenue offsets business — but it sells **matchmaking and intelligence**, not an obligation ledger. Proof that industry *will* pay a subscription in this category. |
| **Eurostep ShareAspace** | PLM/controlled-data-sharing platform repositioned into offsets messaging ([Eurostep](https://eurostep.com/defense-offsets-from-contractual-burden-to-competitive-weapon/)) | Not an offset product. No credits, multipliers, milestones or LD logic. Content marketing. |
| **IOCorp (International Offset Corporation)** | Takes on offset obligations as **principal** and sources goods/services to discharge them ([iocorp.org](https://www.iocorp.org/offsets-2)) | A services/fulfilment house. No software. |
| **Avascent** | Offsets analysis practice; **acquired by Oliver Wyman, October 2022** | Consultancy, now inside a Big-3-adjacent firm. Structurally opposed to software that shrinks billable days. |
| **Blenheim Capital Services** | FSA-regulated offset structured-finance broker; **ceased operations** per PitchBook | Instructive: the most financially sophisticated player in the space is gone. |
| **Government-side portals** | India's **DOMW portal** handles offset discharge claims and credit assignment ([domw.gov.in](https://domw.gov.in/)); Turkey's **SSB** requires items to be registered in the **YETEN** portal to count ([SSB](https://www.ssb.gov.tr/en/industrial-participation-offset-)); Switzerland's **armasuisse** publishes a public offset register | **The buyer-nation side is being built by governments themselves.** Do not plan to sell the authority side. |
| **ERP modules** | None. Searched SAP, Oracle, IFS. No industrial-participation or offset module exists. | Genuine gap. |
| **Generic CLM** | Icertis, Agiloft, Aavenir Obligationflow, LinkSquares all now market "contract obligation management" | **Your real competitive threat.** My company already owns a CLM. When I ask for offset software, Legal will tell me to use a module of the thing we already pay for. It will be terrible. It will also be free to me. |

**So: is it whitespace or a graveyard? It is whitespace with graves in it.** The honest reasons no SaaS has won:

1. **The buyer count is brutally small.** BIS recorded **1,304 offset agreements across 51 countries over 29 years** ([BIS 28th Study](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/public-version-28th-annual-offsets-report.pdf)). That is roughly 45 new agreements a year globally, US firms included. Maybe 200–400 firms worldwide carry obligations; maybe 60–80 have a function big enough to fund software. At £100k average that is a **£6–8m ARR ceiling**. That is a good consultancy. It is not a venture outcome unless you expand into adjacent local-content/ICV regimes.
2. **Every country's rules are different and they move.** Turkey revised its guideline in 2011 and again in 2022; Poland abolished indirect offsets entirely; UAE raised its trigger; Greece is running a 25% rule that is still only administrative guidance. A product that encodes rules must maintain 25+ rulebooks forever. That maintenance cost is the moat *and* the reason nobody has built it.
3. **The consultants defend the revenue, and they own the relationships.** NyRad has been in "almost every Government of Canada Value Proposition since 2014". PwC India owns the Indian OEM relationships. They are not going to refer you in.
4. **The obligations are commercially and politically explosive.** See §4.
5. **The category name is unsearchable.** "Offset management software" returns printing-industry MIS. You cannot buy the keyword, and your buyer cannot find you.
6. **The pain is misdiagnosed.** Tracking is annoying; *discharging* is the job. A VP of Offsets is judged on whether he found a local partner willing to take the work at a 3x multiplier — and Epicos, IOCorp and the brokers exist precisely because that is where the value is. Your ledger makes his reporting nicer. It does not retire his obligation.

## 4. Would I put offset data in your SaaS? Honestly: no, and here is what it would take

This section could kill the idea and should be read as such.

**The four blockers, in order of severity:**

1. **It is a discoverable record of my own breach.** An offset ledger is, by construction, a timestamped, structured, centrally-hosted record of the extent to which I am failing to meet obligations owed to a sovereign customer. India's own Parliamentary review found **~45% of obligations unfulfilled as of December 2025** ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/management-of-defence-offsets)). My General Counsel's advice will be one sentence: *do not create that artefact outside our control.* **Blenheim Capital Holdings v. Lockheed Martin** — offset brokerage litigation reaching the Fourth Circuit and a denied cert petition ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/21-2104/21-2104-2022-11-15.html)) — is exactly the scenario where that artefact gets subpoenaed.

2. **Offsets are a named corruption-risk category.** Transparency International rates offsets as high or very-high corruption risk in Indonesia, Malaysia, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, UAE and the UK, and says the US approach "effectively leaves defence firms to mark their own homework" ([TI-Defence](https://ti-defence.org/blissfully-blind-security-risks-defence-contract-offset/)). Offset discharge runs through local partners and intermediaries — the canonical FCPA/Bribery Act fact pattern. A third-party system holding my agent payments and credit claims is an investigator's dream.

3. **ITAR.** Technology transfer was **14.39% of offset transaction value in 2022** ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/public-version-28th-annual-offsets-report.pdf)). Descriptions of those transfers can be ITAR technical data. Putting them into a SaaS whose administrators are not US persons is an unauthorised export. This is not a policy preference; it is a criminal exposure, and it applies to your hosting provider's support staff too.

4. **My customer's own confidentiality clauses.** Offset agreements with authorities routinely bind the obligor to confidentiality. I may not be contractually permitted to disclose obligation terms to a processor at all without the authority's consent — and no offset authority has ever approved a third-party SaaS processor.

**What it would actually take (all of these, not some):**

- **Single tenant, my encryption key, vendor blind to values.** If you can read my obligation balances, you are a party to them.
- **Per-jurisdiction residency, enforced.** UAE obligations in a UAE tenant, India in India. Your platform doc already notes jurisdiction binding is immutable at creation — good, and architecturally brutal, because offsets need more jurisdictions than `eu`/`fedramp` offer.
- **US-person-only administrative access,** evidenced, with a technology control plan.
- **A metadata-only schema.** Obligation value, milestone date, counterparty name, credit claimed, multiplier applied — **never** the technical content of a transfer, never agent payments. This is the single design decision that makes the product legally buildable.
- **A peer reference.** Nobody goes first on this. Get a mid-tier Nordic or Israeli exporter to go first and say so publicly.
- **An export-controlled data warranty with real liability,** not a 12-month-fees cap.

Realistically that is a 24–36 month enterprise sale per logo. **Which is precisely why you should not start there.**

## 6. Which candidate needs no customer permission

This is the question that decides your sales velocity, and the answers are cleaner than expected.

- **Offset rulebook / bid-time exposure model — zero permission.** You are selling me *published national rules*, not touching my customer's data. No InfoSec review, no DPA, no authority consent. **Fastest of all five.**
- **FOCI — zero permission.** I file my own SF 328. Nobody blesses my preparation tool.
- **Readiness passport — permission-dependent in a subtle way.** A *preparation and evidence-reuse* tool needs nobody. A *passport* — a credential others must accept — is worthless without MOD/prime blessing, and that blessing already belongs to JOSCAR and IASME. **Build the preparation tool, never claim to be the passport.**
- **Offset post-award ledger — permission-heavy.** GC, export control, InfoSec, and arguably the offset authority.
- **Munitions capacity — entirely permission-gated.** Needs government compulsion to obtain sub-tier data.
- **Inventory — government is the customer.** All permission, all the time.

## 7. The 90-day wedge

The smallest thing that gets a PO from me inside 90 days has four properties: it comes out of a budget that already exists, it sits under a single manager's delegated authority (£25–50k UK, $10–25k US), it processes **no customer data and no PII** so it skips InfoSec entirely, and it demonstrably affects whether we win.

**The wedge is the offset rulebook, not the offset ledger.**

Concretely: a maintained, **date-versioned** database of every offset and industrial-participation regime — thresholds, ratio, eligible activity categories, multiplier tables, banking and credit-expiry rules, LD and bank-guarantee formulas, claim-submission mechanics (DOMW portal, SSB YETEN, Tawazun, ITB/VP, AIC, LCGPA) — wrapped in a bid-time exposure calculator: *"if I bid this campaign in this country at this value, here is my obligation, my discharge runway, my LD exposure and my guarantee cost."*

Why this wins in 90 days:
- It is sold to a **bid director in a live campaign with a submission date**. Urgency is supplied by my customer, not by your marketing.
- It touches **none of my sensitive data** — it is a content subscription with a calculator, the shape defence people already buy without a procurement ceremony.
- It is **£20–35k**, inside my signature.
- Per your own platform research, date-versioned regulatory snapshots are the compounding asset a later competitor cannot backfill. Offset rulebooks are exactly that, and there is no OpenSanctions-style free alternative to undercut you. **Start the snapshot cron on day one.**
- It earns the right to the ledger. Once I trust your rulebook, the conversation about hosting my obligation balances becomes a renewal discussion rather than a cold enterprise sale.

Secondary wedge if you want SME volume instead of ACV: the **DCC Level 0 readiness pack** at £299–£999/yr, credit card, against the **31 December 2026** deadline. Thousands of buyers, no permission needed, but it is a different company with a different cost structure. Pick one.

## 8. What I would buy tomorrow

- **As VP of Offsets at a tier-1:** the country rulebook and exposure model, **£25k**, on a requisition I approve myself, this week. I would **not** buy a hosted obligation ledger — not without single-tenancy, my own key, a metadata-only schema, a US-person access control plan, and a named peer already live.
- **As bid director at a mid-tier:** the same, plus an offset-commitment red-team before we sign anything in the Gulf. **£40k.** The thing that scares me is committing to 60% of contract value with an 8.5% on-demand guarantee because nobody modelled the discharge runway.
- **As MD of a 40-person SME:** the DCC/readiness evidence pack at **£500–£1,500/yr** on a card, today, because 31 December 2026 costs me the right to bid. I will not buy anything above £2k that I could plausibly do myself in a weekend.
- **The two I would tell you to build behind it:** FOCI the week the DFARS rule is final — best liability shape in the set — and the offset ledger in year two, to customers who already pay you for the rulebook.
- **The two I would not build:** munitions capacity modelling (right pain, no reachable buyer, unobtainable data) and the inventory layer (no industry buyer exists at all).

**One-line summary for the team:** offsets is genuinely unserved and the incumbents are a bespoke PwC India app, an unlaunched NyRad/Contextere "offset co-pilot", a matchmaking portal called Epicos, and a wall of consultancies — but the confidential half of the problem is unsellable to a startup in year one, and the unconfidential half is sellable next quarter. **Sell the rules, not the ledger.**
