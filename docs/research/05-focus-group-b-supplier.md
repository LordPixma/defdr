# Focus Group B — "The Supplier": a UK industry verdict on the DEFDR candidates

**Author:** Focus Group Agent B (composite: VP Offsets/Industrial Participation at a UK-HQ or UK-operating exporter — BAE Systems, Babcock, Rolls-Royce, Thales UK, Leonardo UK, MBDA UK, QinetiQ; a bid director at a UK mid-tier; the MD of a 40-person UK SME trying to get into the MOD supply chain)
**Date:** 19 September 2026
**Scope:** UK market (revised from global per Orchestrator scope change)
**Bias declaration:** I do not care about your architecture. I care about winning bids, cash, headcount, and not personally signing something that gets me sued or debarred. I will pay for software that makes me money or removes a liability from my signature. I will not pay for software that creates work for my team.

## 1. Verdict and ranking — UK

Narrowing to the UK does two things to the offsets thesis. It **strengthens** the outbound half and it **creates a second, better product** on the inbound half. It also **kills the readiness passport outright**, and it **demotes FOCI**.

The asymmetry the Orchestrator flagged is the whole ballgame: a UK exporter's offset obligations are owed to *foreign* buyer nations, but the obligation-holder, the signatory, the budget and the liability are all **in the UK**. And 2025 was the biggest year in UK history for creating exactly those obligations: **UK defence exports exceeded £20bn, the highest since records began**, led by a **£10bn Norwegian Type 26 deal** and an **£8bn Typhoon sale to Türkiye**, with Saudi Arabia at **£4.5bn** and Qatar at **£3.9bn** ([UK Defence Journal](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/uk-defence-exports-hit-record-20bn-in-2025/); [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/news/uk-defence-exports-reach-26-9bn/)). Türkiye's SSB requires **a minimum of 70% of contract value** in industrial participation; Saudi GAMI is driving **50% localisation of military spend by 2030**; Norway negotiates separate industrial cooperation agreements above **NOK 50m**; UAE is **60% plus an 8.5% on-demand bank guarantee**. **UK plc booked more offset liability in 2025 than in any year of its history.** That is the UK-specific case, and it is strong.

|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Outbound offsets — pre-award only** (country rulebook + bid-time exposure model) | **Yes, tomorrow, £20–30k, on my own delegated authority** | Campaign/B&P budget, zero customer data, no InfoSec review, directly affects whether we win. Record obligation year = live campaigns now. |

| **4** | **FOCI / beneficial-ownership workflow** | **Marginal in UK scope** | It is a *US* rule. Only relevant to the ~15–25 UK groups with US subsidiaries above $5m. The UK analogue (NSIA) is deal-by-deal law-firm work, not recurring software. |

**Headline:** offsets is a genuine UK whitespace. But the UK buyer count for the outbound product is **20–30 logos**, and you must know that before you commit. The volume is on the inbound side, which the UK is about to create from scratch.

*Tier 2 — one person, part-time, or a bid-director side job:* Chemring, Ultra, Cobham Mission Systems, Martin-Baker, Marshall Aerospace (the **£550m+ C-130 sale to Türkiye**), Supacat, Pearson Engineering, NP Aerospace, GKN Aerospace, Meggitt/Parker, Serco, RBSL, Team Tempest partners.

*Foreign-owned UK operations that carry UK-content obligations:* Lockheed Martin UK, Boeing Defence UK, General Dynamics UK, Elbit Systems UK, Raytheon UK, Leidos UK, KBR.

**Realistic UK buyer count — the number the product owner needs:**

- **UK firms with a real, funded offset/IP function: 8–12.**
- **UK firms with any offset obligation at all: 25–40.**
- **People in the UK whose job title contains "offset" or "industrial participation": roughly 80–150**, of whom perhaps **30–50** sit in a named role with budget influence. BAE alone is probably 15–30 of them, and many of those sit in-country (Riyadh, Doha, Canberra), not in the UK.
- **Genuinely addressable UK logos for an outbound offsets product: 20–30.**

**So-what, stated plainly:** at £25–50k per logo, a UK-only outbound offsets product has an **ARR ceiling of roughly £0.75m–£1.5m**. That is a consultancy, not a software company. The product only becomes venture-scale if it (a) follows UK firms into their obligation countries, or (b) captures the **inbound** UK regime, where the buyer population is an order of magnitude larger.

## 3. Q2 — The SME MD on the 5% / 3% / 75% problem

I am the MD of a 40-person engineering firm in the West Midlands. Here is my honest answer.

**What I will not pay for:** another tender feed. DCI/Supply2Defence, Stotles and Tussell already exist, and my problem has never been that I cannot *see* opportunities. My problem is that **75% of MOD's SME spend flows through primes** and I have no route to the person inside a prime who decides. A dashboard showing me contracts I cannot win is a subscription I cancel in month four.

**What I will pay for:** a named human and a reason for them to call me back. Specifically:

1. **Demand signal tied to an obligation.** If a foreign prime is contractually obliged to place UK work — which is exactly what the "Back British" regime is designed to create, with **UK job creation, UK workshare and use of UK SMEs in supply chains** named as key components ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/uk-gov-launches-consultation-on-an-offsets-regime-for-the-defence-sector-time-to-102lvvx)) — then for the first time in my career, someone has a *contractual reason* to find me. **Being findable against a specific obligation is worth money to me in a way that being listed on a directory never has been.**
2. **Pre-qualification I have already done, presented in the form the prime's IP manager needs.** Not a new certificate — a translation of what I already hold.
3. **The £2.5bn target made legible.** MOD has committed to **+£2.5bn of SME spend by summer 2028**. I want to know which primes are behind on their aligned action plans, because that is where my leverage is.

**Price:** **£600–£1,800/yr on a company credit card.** Above £2k I will ask my ops manager to do it in a spreadsheet, and he will. Below £600 I will not believe it works.

**The killer feature:** free to list, paid to be *ranked and verified*. Get 2,000 UK SMEs on the supply side for nothing before the regime lands, then charge the foreign primes who need to discharge against them. The SMEs are the asset; the primes are the revenue.

## 4. Q3–Q4 — Is the readiness passport real? No. Here is why, plainly.

### 4.1 DCC Level 0 is a form, not a software problem

I checked the actual numbers before answering. **DCC Level 0 certification starts from £650 + VAT, with certification bodies quoting fixed prices from £799 + VAT** ([Vincent Cyber Defence](https://vincentcyberdefence.co.uk/blog/dcc-level-0-explained/); [IASME FAQs](https://iasme.co.uk/defence-cyber-certification/frequently-asked-questions/)). The scheme is delivered through **IASME's nationwide network of assured Certification Bodies**, IASME is **the MOD's official cyber certification partner**, and the scheme passed its first anniversary in May 2026 ([Defence Digital blog](https://defencedigital.blog.gov.uk/2026/05/08/one-year-of-defence-cyber-certification-building-stronger-cyber-resilience-together/)). Level 0 is assigned "where there is a very low level of assessed cyber risk."

**As the SME MD: I will fill this in once, pay £650–£799, and never think about it again.** I am not buying an annual SaaS subscription to help me complete a one-off assessment that costs £650 and is administered by an accredited body that will hold my hand through it. Any vendor entering here is competing against a £650 one-off *and* against the certification bodies themselves, who are incentivised to bundle the preparation for free to win the assessment fee.

The "dozens of certification bodies against thousands of suppliers" framing in the landscape doc is a **capacity** observation, not a **software** opportunity. Capacity shortages get solved by more assessors, not by a SaaS.

### 4.2 JOSCAR already is the UK readiness passport — this kills candidate 3

Stated plainly, as requested: **yes, JOSCAR solves this for the UK, and the idea should be dropped.**

- **Coverage:** nearly **40 buying organisations** across UK and Australia, **including the Ministry of Defence itself**, plus BAE Systems, Babcock, Leonardo UK, MBDA, General Dynamics UK, Lockheed Martin, Boeing, Leidos UK, Elbit Systems UK, AtkinsRéalis, Cammell Laird, AirTanker, Draken and NATS ([Hellios buyer community](https://hellios.com/buyer-community-members-joscar)).
- **Scope of data:** company structure, financial standing, insurance, quality systems, health and safety, environmental, **cyber security**, modern slavery and wider governance — i.e. essentially the entire "evidence graph" candidate 3 proposed to build.
- **Mechanism:** one registration, held centrally, revalidated annually, visible to all buying members. That is the definition of a passport.
- **Price:** Stage 1 free; Stage 2 **free below £1m turnover, £725 + VAT/yr above** ([Hellios](https://hellios.com/supplier-payment-info-joscar/)).

A passport is only worth anything if the people at the gate accept it. The gate-keepers here are MOD and every UK prime, and they already accept JOSCAR. A rival passport would need MOD and ten primes to bless it — the definition of a product that sells 10x slower. **Do not build this.** If there is a residual play it is *inside* the Hellios ecosystem as a preparation tool, which is a feature, not a company.

## 5. Q5 answered in passing, then the honest offsets question

### 5.1 The Kahootz anchor is the most important pricing fact in this document

Kahootz sells MOD-assessed **OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE** collaboration on G-Cloud at **£3.69–£11.69 per user per month** — £44 to £140 per user per year ([Digital Marketplace](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/863966228041800)).

**So-what: never price this product per seat in the UK.** My offsets function is three people. At even a heroic £500/user/year that is £1,500 — not a business. Worse, the moment you quote per-user, a UK defence procurement officer will benchmark you against £140/user/yr and refuse. **Price per obligation, per campaign, or per company.** The unit of value is an obligation worth tens of millions, not a login.

### 5.2 Why has no SaaS won in offsets? Named incumbents, honestly

I searched for an offset management SaaS the way a buyer would. Here is everything that exists.

**Whitespace or graveyard? Whitespace with graves in it.** The honest reasons:

1. **The buyer count is tiny** — globally, BIS recorded **1,304 offset agreements across 51 countries over 29 years** ([BIS 28th Study](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/public-version-28th-annual-offsets-report.pdf)), roughly 45 a year. In the UK, 20–30 logos (§2).
2. **Every country's rules differ and move.** Türkiye revised its guideline in 2011 and again 2022; Poland abolished indirect offsets; UAE raised its trigger; Greece's 25% rule is still only administrative guidance. Maintaining 25+ rulebooks forever is the moat *and* the reason nobody built it.
3. **Consultants defend the revenue and own the relationships.** NyRad, PwC India, CORE, MMC, Cranfield Defence Management Lab. They will not refer you in.
4. **The data is commercially and politically explosive** (§5.3).
5. **The category name is unsearchable.** "Offset management software" returns printing-industry MIS. You cannot buy the keyword and your buyer cannot find you.
6. **The pain is misdiagnosed.** Tracking is annoying; *discharging* is the job. My bonus depends on finding a Turkish or Norwegian partner who will take the work at a 3x multiplier. Epicos, IOCorp and the brokers exist because that is where the value is. **Your ledger makes my reporting nicer. It does not retire my obligation.**

### 5.3 Would I put offset data in your SaaS? Honestly, no — and here is what it would take

This section can kill the idea and should be read that way.

1. **It is a discoverable record of my own breach.** India's own Parliamentary review (**report of 22 July 2026**) found **~45% of offset obligations unfulfilled as of December 2025**, with penalties of **5% of the unfulfilled obligation**, and criticised "non-discharge of offset obligations, submission of incorrect offset claims, and slow pace of verification" ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/management-of-defence-offsets)). My General Counsel's advice will be one sentence: *do not create that artefact outside our control.* **Blenheim Capital Holdings v. Lockheed Martin** — UK-linked offset brokerage litigation that reached the US Fourth Circuit and a denied cert petition ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/21-2104/21-2104-2022-11-15.html)) — is exactly the scenario where that artefact gets subpoenaed.
2. **Offsets are a named corruption-risk category, and the UK is on the list.** Transparency International rates offsets high or very-high corruption risk in Indonesia, Malaysia, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, UAE **and the UK** ([TI-Defence](https://ti-defence.org/blissfully-blind-security-risks-defence-contract-offset/)). Discharge runs through local partners and intermediaries — the canonical Bribery Act 2010 fact pattern. A UK company under a section 7 failure-to-prevent analysis does not want a third party holding that ledger.
3. **ITAR, and it bites UK firms hardest.** Technology transfer was **14.39% of global offset transaction value in 2022** ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/public-version-28th-annual-offsets-report.pdf)). Descriptions of those transfers can be ITAR technical data. A UK-hosted SaaS with non-US-person administrators is an unauthorised export. This is a criminal exposure, and it reaches your hosting provider's support staff.
4. **My customer's confidentiality clauses.** Offset agreements with GAMI, Tawazun, SSB and DOMW routinely bind me to confidentiality. I may not be permitted to disclose terms to a processor at all, and no offset authority has approved a third-party SaaS.

**What it would take — all of these, not some:** single tenant with my own encryption key and you blind to values; per-jurisdiction residency; US-person-only administrative access with a technology control plan; **a metadata-only schema** (obligation value, milestone date, counterparty, credit claimed, multiplier applied — never the technical content, never agent payments); a named peer reference, because nobody goes first; and an export-controlled data warranty with real liability, not a 12-month-fees cap. That is a 24–36 month enterprise sale per logo, against 20–30 UK logos. **Which is precisely why you must not start there.**

## 7. Which candidate needs no customer permission

This decides sales velocity, and in UK scope the answers are unusually clean.

- **Offset rulebook / bid-time exposure model — zero permission.** You are selling me *published foreign national rules*. No customer data, no InfoSec review, no DPA, no authority consent. **Fastest of all.**
- **Inbound UK marketplace, SME side — zero permission.** An SME listing itself needs nobody's blessing.
- **Inbound UK marketplace, prime side — partial.** Foreign primes will want to know MOD tolerates it. They will not need MOD to mandate it.
- **Post-award ledger — permission-heavy.** GC, export control, InfoSec, arguably the foreign offset authority.
- **Readiness passport — fatally permission-dependent.** Worthless without MOD and prime acceptance, and that acceptance already belongs to JOSCAR and IASME.
- **Munitions capacity and inventory — entirely permission-gated**, government-sponsored, slow.

## 8. The 90-day wedge

The smallest thing that gets a PO from a UK buyer in 90 days has four properties: it comes from a budget that already exists; it sits under one manager's delegated authority (**£25–50k** at a UK prime); it processes **no customer data and no PII**, so it skips InfoSec entirely; and it demonstrably affects whether we win.

**The wedge is the offset rulebook, not the offset ledger.**

A maintained, **date-versioned** database of every foreign offset and industrial-participation regime a UK exporter meets — thresholds, ratios, eligible activity categories, multiplier tables, banking and credit-expiry rules, LD and bank-guarantee formulas, and claim-submission mechanics (GAMI/LCGPA, Tawazun, SSB YETEN, DOMW, Norwegian ICAs, Polish Offset Act, ITB/VP, AIC) — wrapped in a bid-time exposure calculator: *"if we bid this campaign in Türkiye at £800m, here is the 70% obligation, the discharge runway, the LD exposure and the guarantee cost."*

Why it wins in 90 days:
- Sold to a **UK bid director in a live campaign with a submission date**. Urgency supplied by my customer, not your marketing. After a record £20bn export year, those campaigns exist right now.
- Touches **none of my sensitive data** — a content subscription with a calculator, the shape UK defence already buys without procurement ceremony.
- **£20–30k**, inside my signature, priced per company rather than per seat so it never meets the Kahootz benchmark.
- Per your own platform research, date-versioned regulatory snapshots are the compounding asset a later competitor cannot backfill, and offset rulebooks have no open-licensed free alternative to undercut you. **Start the snapshot cron on day one.**
- It earns the right to the ledger. Once I trust your rulebook, hosting my balances becomes a renewal conversation, not a cold enterprise sale.

**Run in parallel, at near-zero cost:** open free SME listings on the inbound UK side now, ahead of the regime. Rupert Pearce as National Armaments Director is charged with implementing it, and Luke Pollard reconfirmed intent at DPRTE on **25 March 2026** ([Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/uk-consultation-back-british-defence-offset-plan.html)). When foreign primes need UK SMEs to discharge against, the side with 2,000 profiles already built wins the market. The SMEs are the asset; the primes are the revenue.

I would **not** buy a hosted obligation ledger without single-tenancy, my own key, a metadata-only schema, a US-person access control plan, and a named peer already live.
- **As bid director at a UK mid-tier:** the same, plus an offset-commitment red-team before we sign anything in the Gulf or Türkiye. **£40k.** What frightens me is committing to 60–70% of contract value with an 8.5% on-demand guarantee because nobody modelled the discharge runway at bid time.
- **As MD of a 40-person UK SME:** a verified profile on the inbound offset marketplace at **£600–1,800/yr** on a card — because for the first time someone will have a contractual reason to call me. I would **not** buy DCC tooling (£650 one-off) or a rival to JOSCAR (£725 and MOD already accepts it).
- **Build behind it, in order:** the inbound UK marketplace prime side; then the post-award ledger in year two, to customers who already pay for the rulebook.

**One line for the team:** UK exporters have just booked a record £20bn of exports into the heaviest offset regimes on earth, there is no offset SaaS anywhere — the incumbents are a bespoke PwC India app, an unlaunched NyRad/Contextere "co-pilot", a matchmaking portal called Epicos, and a wall of consultancies — but only 20–30 UK logos exist for the outbound product and the confidential half is unsellable cold. **Sell the rules, not the ledger; and get the UK SME side of the new inbound regime built before the regime is.**
