## 2. BUYER A — Finance Director, UK recruitment agency (S1)

*£62m turnover, ~2,400 contractors out, ~1,700 paid through six umbrellas.*

**1. Buy? £0. No.** Not because I lack the problem — I have it acutely. Because you are selling the
wrong half, and the right half is already on the market.

HMRC's guidance is unambiguous: *"The agency or end client is responsible for making sure PAYE is
operated correctly… We can recover any underpayment of PAYE from them."* I fetched it looking for a
due-diligence defence. **There is none.** No reasonable-care safe harbour, no accreditation
discharge, no indemnity protection.

So in July I went looking for a fix and found **Fortis**, from Professional Passport. Their pitch is
the argument I would otherwise have had to make internally:

> *"Accreditation, due diligence, payslip reviews and RTI checks remain important, but they are
> fundamentally detection tools… they do not control the funds or ensure that the PAYE attributable
> to your workers reaches HMRC."*

Fortis takes one upload across my whole umbrella chain, calculates payroll **worker-by-worker on
HMRC-recognised payroll software**, **separates the payment**, releases net to the umbrella,
**settles PAYE directly to HMRC against the umbrella's own PAYE reference**, and hands me an audit
trail by worker, umbrella and pay run. It is sold explicitly to *"agencies, MSPs and end clients"* —
the buyer ADR-011 calls unserved.

S1 reconciles rate → payslip → RTI → remittance. That is detection. It tells me, after the money is
gone, that an umbrella I can no longer sue hasn't paid tax I now owe. Professional Passport has
already published the headline that kills it — *"Monthly RTI Checks are no guarantee"* — then built
the thing that is. Absent Fortis I'd pay ~**£8,000/yr** (£4–5 per umbrella worker). Nowhere near
ADR-011's own £50k floor.

**2. Budget line.** Compliance & professional fees, central overhead — same line as audit fee, REC
membership, employment lawyer. **Opex.** Not the liability line: that is a contingent, unbudgeted
provision and you cannot spend against a provision not yet raised.

**3. Who signs.** Me to £25k. £25k–£75k: MD plus a one-page case. Above £75k: board, and for a
PE-backed group above £100k, investment committee. At £8k I sign in a meeting. **At £50k I'm writing
a paper defending why I didn't just buy Fortis.**

**4. Today.** Specifically: cut the PSL from fourteen umbrellas to six in February. Collect FCSA /
Professional Passport certificates at onboarding, re-check annually, track expiry in a spreadsheet.
Accountants' supply-chain review in May, ~£11,000 one-off. Indemnity and information-sharing clauses
in every umbrella contract. Monthly RTI confirmations since April, which arrive late and which
nobody on my team is qualified to interrogate. **I have bought reassurance, not assurance.**

**5. Three objections.**
1. **HMRC's impact note caps you.** Continuing annual compliance cost: **£21.7m across ~30,000
   agencies** = **~£723 per agency per year**. Concentrate 70% of it into the top 4,000 and it's
   ~£3,800. The regulator has told my finance committee what this costs. I will read it to you.
2. **Detection arrives too late to be worth money.** By the time you flag a gap, the umbrella has my
   money and HMRC has my number.
3. **My risk is concentrated, not distributed.** Six umbrellas; my exposure is one insolvency. I
   mitigate by consolidating further, or by moving contractors onto my own PAYE — which I'm already
   modelling, and which deletes you entirely.

**6. Existing supplier? Yes — and they already have.** First call **Professional Passport** (Fortis
exists; they accredit four of my six). Second **SafeRec** — I have a free account, and its platform
already names *"end hirers"* as users. Third my front/back office, **Bullhorn** or **Access
Recruitment**, because assignment rates live there and the reconciliation is a join on their data.

**7. Must or should.** **Must do *something*, not must buy *this*.** I don't get fired for buying the
wrong assurance tool. I get fired for having bought none.

**Specific questions.** *Do I rely on accreditation?* I did. I no longer believe it discharges
anything — **and neither does FCSA**, whose own April 2026 note says accreditation *"is not a
substitute for an agency's own due diligence."* They have disclaimed the thing I was buying.
*Would I just write an indemnity and move on — so why buy software?* The indemnity is already in
every contract. It is worth whatever the umbrella's balance sheet is worth on the day HMRC calls,
which for a phoenixing mini-umbrella is nothing. **That is precisely why software can exist — but it
must stop the money, not count it afterwards.** *Do I know I'm liable? Has my accountant told me?*
Yes — partner-signed letter in March, £11k review, webinar invitation. **Awareness is not the gap.
Control of funds is the gap, and it is taken.**

## 3. BUYER B — Asset & Compliance Director, housing association (S2)

*31,000 homes, nine communal/district networks serving ~2,900 homes.*

**1. Buy? Not at £25,000. £4,500/yr per organisation, unlimited networks — and argue me into it.**
£25k in my world is a damp-and-mould surveyor for a year, or ~90 Awaab's Law investigations, or a
fifth of a fire-door programme on one block. Same envelope.

**2. Budget line.** Asset compliance opex — the "big six plus" budget carrying gas, fire, water,
asbestos, lifts, electrical and now damp and mould. Not capital, so not a project budget.
**Part-recoverable via the heat charge** in principle — your one good argument. But I will not add a
compliance-software line to a heat bill in 2026, when my residents already pay more for heat than
their gas-heated neighbours and the Ofgem standards I'm registering under are *about fair pricing*.
That is a Housing Ombudsman case with a bow on it.

**3. Who signs.** Me to £25k under standing orders. £25k–£100k: three written quotes plus Director
sign-off. Above ~£100k: formal tender, and as a contracting authority under the Procurement Act 2023
a services contract above roughly **£214k** goes to open market. **£25k is the worst possible price
point** — big enough that Finance asks what it displaces, small enough that nobody senior fights for
it.

**4. Today.** A named manager in the energy team at 0.3 FTE, no new headcount, with a spreadsheet of
nine networks, meter estates and tariff build-ups. A bought document pack for registration. An
outsourced billing bureau holding customer data. A plant-room condition survey done last year for
another reason, re-used. **We will register before 26 January 2027 and be broadly fine.**

**5. Three objections.**
1. **The market has already set the price and it isn't £25,000.** `heatnetwork-compliance.co.uk`
   sells the 14-document pack covering **every authorisation condition** at **£1,195 + VAT one-off**,
   and **AXIS** — a live, append-only, timestamped register for complaints, water tests, meter checks
   and staff training — at **£895/yr founders, £1,495/yr standard**. Both **per organisation, not per
   network, up to 50 users.** That is your consumer-standards evidence pillar, built, priced, and
   explicitly not per network. You need 17× that. I'll send you the link.
2. **Ofgem is building it and says so.** Its launch blog calls the digital service *"the primary
   means for heat networks to interact with Ofgem and demonstrate compliance"*, in a tone of *"we
   expect to learn together and adapt over time."* That is a regulator saying year one is not an
   enforcement year. No published enforcement action exists against any operator — your LAW 1b score
   of 5 is doing work the evidence doesn't yet support.
3. **Queue position — brutally, as asked.** Building safety cases and remediation; **Awaab's Law
   Phase 2**, live this year with NHF running member webinars; damp and mould; revised Decent Homes;
   MEES; stock condition data. **Heat networks are seventh.** Nobody dies of them, the Ombudsman
   isn't ruling on them, and my board has never asked me about them once. NHF's news feed carries
   Awaab's Law Phase 2 and heat networks nowhere — and NHF has asked government to **pause HNTAS**
   because the sector can't absorb it alongside Decent Homes and MEES. My own trade body is saying
   slow this down.

**6. Existing supplier? Yes, and I'd expect it free.** **Switch2** first — they bill my residents,
already publish on Ofgem reporting readiness, and hold the meter and consumption data that is most
of an A09 return. Then **Insite Energy**. Then my HMS vendor — **MRI**, **Civica** or **Aareon** —
because the complaints log, priority-services flags and service-charge build-up already sit there,
and the vulnerability register is special-category data I'd rather not hand a fourth party.
**If Switch2 adds A09 reporting at renewal, your company ends.** Their data, their contract, their
renewal conversation. That is the base case, not a risk.

**7. Must or should.** **Registration is a must do** — miss 26 January 2027 and it goes in the board
assurance report and possibly to the RSH. But registration is a form, and £1,195 of templates plus
0.3 FTE clears it. **The must-do is small and cheap; the expensive part is the should-do.** Nothing
happens to me personally if my A09 evidence is merely adequate.

**Specific questions.** *How many networks; does per-network pricing work?* Nine — and **no**. Nine ×
£2,500 = £22,500 to manage 2,900 of my 31,000 homes: my cost-per-home for heat compliance would be
8× my cost-per-home for fire safety. The moment you show me that table I stop the meeting. Price per
organisation or per home, and accept a small ACV. **The landlords with 40+ networks are exactly the
ones large enough to already have an in-house energy team.** *£25k or a £4k problem for an existing
supplier?* It is a £4k problem, and it will end up with an existing supplier.

## 4. BUYER C — Head of Packaging, large packaging producer (S3)

*£1.9bn turnover FMCG; EPR disposal fee ~£2.4m in 2026-27, modelled ~£4.6m by 2028-29.*

**1. Buy? Yes in principle — at £18,000–£25,000.** Not £50,000, and not before asking my scheme why
they aren't giving it to me.

The economics are real. **RAM v2 lands for the 1 October 2027 deadline** and is materially harsher:
PFAS becomes a measurable 1ppm threshold rather than an intent test; heat-in-pack fibre goes Red;
non-removable labels and trigger sprays can turn a rigid plastic Red; only clear or light-blue PET
escapes Red; flexibles are capped at Amber and can never be Green. The Red penalty runs **1.2×
(2026-27) → 1.6× → 2.0× (2028-29)**. Every rule change is a P&L line, and I find out which ones bit
me *after* I report. Pain is real, pain is cash, pain compounds. Hence the yes.

**2. Budget line.** **Packaging development opex** — the line funding material trials, specification
work, artwork management. Explicitly **not** the EPR fee line: that sits in Finance as a levy
accrual, and Finance won't release from it, because an accrual is a cost you're reducing, not a
budget you spend. I've tried. **You justify against the fee line and get paid from the development
line** — and the development line is smaller and already committed. Vendors get this wrong
constantly.

**3. Who signs.** Me to £25k within my annual plan. £25k–£100k: Supply Chain Director plus
Procurement, and Procurement insists on three tenders — **so you get benchmarked directly against
Valpak and Ecosurety, whose quotes I can get in a fortnight.** Above £100k: a business case to the
FD with a payback calculation. **At £50k you're in a competitive tender you're structurally set up
to lose.** At £22k you're inside my delegated authority and I buy you in November.

**4. Today.** Two people, a very large spreadsheet, and specification data from our PLM — which is
incomplete for closures, adhesives and colourants, the exact fields RAM turns on. First-pass RAG
across ~1,100 components in the spring; ~140 Red; a change programme on the worst dozen. **We modelled
the fee delta of none of it**, because we have no reliable way to. That is your wedge — and it is
exactly what Valpak already sells.

**5. Three objections.**
1. **Valpak already sells this, by name.** Their RAM page offers consultancy assessment of components
   *or review of in-house assessments*, RAG rating across ranges, **three service tiers from full
   support to light-touch verification**, **"Eco-modulation cost modelling"** for future pEPR fee
   impact, and **"What If Modelling"** for material swaps and design scenarios. That is your product
   description read back to you. Samsung, Coca-Cola, Brother. If they bundle it into membership under
   competitive pressure — and they will — you have no business.
2. **My data isn't good enough for your software to work.** RAM assesses per separable component. My
   PLM has material and weight, not adhesive chemistry, ink coverage, colourant or PFAS content. You
   hand me an implementation project I must staff. Valpak sells me *people* who do that collection:
   worse value per pound, better value per unit of my effort — **and my constraint is my effort.**
3. **I don't control the decision your software informs.** See below.

**6. Existing supplier? My first call is Valpak and it isn't close.** They hold my EPR submission, my
PRN/PERN evidence and my producer registration. Adding RAM is one conversation: no new supplier
onboarding, no DPIA, no security review. Second **Ecosurety** — no RAM on their site, but *"global
packaging data management"*, *"packaging insights and analytics"* and *"guided improvements"* is
close enough that they'll build rather than lose accounts. Third my **PLM vendor**, because a
per-SKU attribute belongs in the system holding the SKU. **The only version of you I buy standalone
is genuinely better software than Valpak's consultancy** — per-component, auto-rescored as the
methodology changes, with scenarios I can run myself at 11pm without raising a service request. If
you're consultancy with a dashboard, I buy Valpak.

**7. Must or should.** **Reporting the RAG rating is a must do** — mandatory field in the
large-producer return. **Optimising it is a should do**, and that sets your ceiling: the must-do is a
data-entry job we already do; you sell into the should-do, judged on payback against every other
margin initiative. If I do nothing, nothing happens to me personally in 2027. In 2029, at 2.0× and
£4.6m, somebody asks why we didn't act three years earlier — and by then I'll have acted, with or
without you.

**Specific questions.** *Do I pay a scheme, and does RAM come with membership?* Yes, Valpak — and
**no, they sell RAM separately, in tiers.** Confirmed. That cuts both ways: it proves producers pay
cash for RAM work beyond membership (real WTP evidence), **and** it proves the incumbent already owns
the commercial motion, the customer and the ability to price to zero the day you threaten them. I
won't disclose my membership fee and neither will they — **no scheme in this market publishes
pricing**, which tells you how little price discovery buyers get. *If they bundle it, would I ever
buy separately?* **Never.** One packaging-compliance supplier is the answer; two is a procurement
problem I must justify. *Who decides design — me or brand?* **Brand and marketing decide, and they
have historically ignored me.** I own specification, cost and compliance; they own shelf appeal, and
"we can't use black PET" is an argument I lose about half the time. **What changed is that the fee
is now a number** — and a number converts an aesthetic argument into a P&L argument in front of the
FD. That's the best thing about your product: build for *that* meeting — a one-page "this design
costs £340k a year more than that one", not an analyst's dashboard. But note what it implies: the
buyer of the output is Finance, the user is me, the decider is a brand manager who will resent the
tool. **Three-party sales are slow.**

## 6. What I would sign tomorrow

**S1 — nothing, at any price, for the product as specified.** I'd sign a Fortis contract instead. The
only S1-shaped thing I'd buy is *settlement*, and Professional Passport has it, with HMRC payroll
recognition I'd have to replicate. **This is a feature, and the feature has been built.** What may
survive is the cross-customer record of which umbrellas actually remit correctly — but that is a data
product sold **to** Fortis and SafeRec, not a company sold to me.

**S2 — £4,500/yr, per organisation, all nine networks, A09 pre-populated from my billing bureau's
feed.** I'd sign that in a week. But 600–900 winnable buyers × £4,500 = **£2.7m–£4.05m of total
category revenue if you win every buyer**, which nobody does. ADR-011 is right that S2 only works at
£25k+; **I am the buyer telling you £25k is not available.** Therefore S2 does not work. Its most
likely home is Switch2's billing contract.

**S3 — £22,000/yr, signed in November, inside my delegated authority**, conditional on: software I
operate rather than consultancy I commission; automatic re-scoring against RAM v2 before October
2027; and the one-page fee-delta comparison for my FD. **That is the only real signature of the
three.** But the arithmetic shifts: **£3m ARR at £22k ACV needs 136 of 1,800–2,500 winnable
customers — 5.4%–7.6% penetration**, against an evidenced 1–3% base case and roughly double what
ADR-011 modelled at £50k. Not impossible — Ideagen reached 3.2% — but not the comfortable 2.4–3.3%
the synthesis claimed, because the synthesis assumed a price I will not pay.

**Ranking: S3 > S2 > S1.** Only S3 clears "company rather than feature", and it clears it narrowly,
against an incumbent already selling the exact service by name.
