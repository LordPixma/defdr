# Conflict Register — unresolved disagreements between team members

Recorded by the Orchestrator before synthesis. Disagreement is adjudicated, not averaged.

## CONFLICT 1 — N-tier supply chain visibility: opportunity or trap?

**Procurement Expert says BUILD IT.** Calls it "the best-triangulated product thesis in the
report." Evidence: NDAA FY24 Section 805 bars DoD from contracting directly with listed entities
from 30 Jun 2026 and **indirectly, anywhere upstream, from 30 Jun 2027** — converting sub-tier
mapping from preference to legal obligation. Plus: 75% of MOD's ~£5bn SME spend flows indirectly
through primes and **MOD cannot see it**.

**Defence Sector Expert says DO NOT BUILD IT.** Lists "US federal supply-chain illumination" as an
explicit trap: the **$919m GSA SCRIPTS BPA** locks in Govini/Exiger/Interos for a decade.

**Orchestrator's read:** these may not actually contradict. SCRIPTS is a *US federal* vehicle for
*illumination as a service to government*. The procurement expert's opening is *UK/prime-side*
compliance evidence, a different buyer with a different budget. But it could equally be two framings
of the same losing fight. **Referred to Focus Group C (Sceptic) for adjudication.** Do not resolve
by preference.

## CONFLICT 2 — Who is the customer?

Sector Expert leans toward buyer-side (ministries, armaments directorates). Procurement Expert is
emphatic that **the ministry is not the year-one buyer** — the prime or an innovation body is, and
anyone forecasting a seven-figure ministry contract in year one "is forecasting a fantasy."

**Orchestrator's read:** Procurement Expert wins on evidence. Any candidate whose only buyer is a
ministry IT department is disqualified for launch, whatever its long-term merit. This becomes a
**selection criterion**, not a conflict to resolve later.

## CONFLICT 3 — Does the offsets convergence mean anything?

Two agents ranked offsets #1 independently. That is either triangulation or shared search bias.
**Referred to Focus Group C.** Until answered, offsets is a *leading candidate*, not the decision.

## CONFLICT 4 — The Cloudflare mandate vs the US market

Cloudflare has FedRAMP High but **not DoD IL4**. The US DoD CUI market is therefore unreachable at
launch. This is not a conflict between agents but between the user's hosting mandate and the largest
defence software market on earth.

**Orchestrator's read:** this is settled and non-negotiable — the mandate is the mandate. Its
consequence is that **the chosen product must not depend on US DoD CUI workloads for its core value
proposition.** That is a hard selection criterion. A candidate that only works at IL4 is dead on
arrival under this brief, however attractive it looks.

---

## Selection criteria derived from the conflicts

A candidate must satisfy ALL of these to be chosen:

1. **Year-one buyer is a prime, an innovation body, or a non-ministry commercial entity** — not a
   ministry IT department. (Conflict 2)
2. **Core value survives without US DoD CUI / IL4.** (Conflict 4)
3. **Not in a space locked by a long-dated incumbent vehicle** (SCRIPTS, Descartes, SiliconExpert).
4. **Data it needs must be obtainable** under open licences or from the customer directly — no
   dependence on JOSCAR, NMCRL, DIBBS, GIDEP or CC BY-NC sources. (Webscraper)
5. **Survives Focus Group C.** No exceptions.
